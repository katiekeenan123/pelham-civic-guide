2026 Pelham Elections

Village of Pelham Board of Trustees · 3 seats · 2-year terms
First contested race since 2019
Context: Hanan Eldahry, who has served since 2018, is not seeking reelection. Fiscal management is a major issue discussed by candidates and covered in local reporting — the village passed a $20.5M budget with a 10.1% increase, overriding the state tax cap for the first time. Three seats are up; top three vote-getters win.
Democratic Party
Russell Solomon
Incumbent · First elected 2020 · Completing third term
Background
A Village of Pelham trustee for six years, first elected in 2020 and now completing his third term. Professionally he is Executive Creative Director at a national firm; he moved to Pelham in 2007 and has raised three children here. As the board’s liaison to the Police Department he pushed for a dedicated Traffic Officer in the village budget, and he cites lowering the village speed limit to 25 mph, redesigning the Wolfs Lane and Sparks Avenue intersection, and the school zone speed camera home rule the Governor signed in August 2026. He says that after he joined the board during COVID, with village revenues in freefall, the board restored the village’s finances and earned an AA+ credit rating from S&P Global. He also points to relining 10% of the village’s sanitary sewers and bringing sanitation in-house to DPW.
Why they say they’re running
He says the trust residents give “only means something if it’s followed through with collaborative work and results.” He calls stormwater remediation a priority, citing residents who have repeatedly seen their basements flood. He notes that this is the first contested Village Trustee election since 2019, and he is running alongside fellow incumbent Krystal Howell and Kristen Burke.
Prior public service
Village of Pelham Board of Trustees, 2020 to present.
Stated priorities
  • Pedestrian safety and traffic calming
  • Fiscal management
  • Sewer and infrastructure repair
  • Stormwater remediation
  • Responds to the Neighborhood Party debt projection: says no bond has been authorized and the $48.5M figure is an evolving estimate, not committed debt. (pelhamexaminer.com/84941, October 2, 2026)
Relevant quotes
The trust you give only means something if it’s followed by the work.
I’ve been fortunate not to experience severe flooding, but I know plenty of Pelham residents who’ve watched water fill their basements more than once, who’ve drained their savings trying to keep their homes livable, and who’ve had to gut and rebuild rooms they’d already rebuilt once before.

Krystal Howell
Incumbent · First elected 2024
Background
Finishing her first term as a Village of Pelham trustee, first elected in 2024. She is a CPA with 25 years of experience in the energy sector, covering construction and project management, contractor oversight, budgeting and financial planning, safety and environmental compliance, and capital program management; she says she has managed large portfolios and supported work exceeding $2 billion a year. As trustee she supported pedestrian safety efforts including the hiring of a Traffic Safety Officer, advocated for the village with other levels of government, and worked with neighboring municipalities on shared quality-of-life issues. Before joining the board she was PTA Council President and served on local PTA boards, the Pelham Together Diversity Committee, the school district’s Cultural Competency Committee, Bridges of Pelham and the Village Steering Committee on Police Reform, and started free drop-in math tutoring at the high school after COVID.
Why they say they’re running
She says she is seeking re-election to continue moving the village forward “with purpose, transparency, and care.” She points to many hours spent with engineers and regional partners on the village’s stormwater challenges, and to her support for the Downtown Improvement Grant to keep Pelham’s small businesses vibrant.

Kristen Burke
Town Council Member · General Counsel, Mercy University
Background
An attorney who has been General Counsel at Mercy University for more than 11 years, after working as Associate Counsel at CUNY and as a Rackets prosecutor in the Bronx. She has served on the Town of Pelham Town Council for six years, as its legal and courts liaison. Her current term runs through 2027, and she has said she would step down from the seat if elected to the Village board. She cites securing and contracting services for Pelham’s own in-house ambulance within a year, after strong community advocacy, as a Town Council accomplishment.

She notes there are no attorneys on the Village board and says her legal experience would help with the complex flood mitigation project, labor and employment matters, contracts and policy review, at a time when the village faces rising costs and funding cuts. She also wants to continue her work on pedestrian safety, including a project she is leading near the Glover Field complex.

Arthur Long
Challenger · Ran in 2019
Background
An attorney at Latham & Watkins who ran for the Village of Pelham board in 2019. He signed the joint Neighborhood Party platform alongside Rhett Speros and Paul Anzilotti.

Democratic Party · Manor Together
Ryan Kurtz
Challenger · Ran in 2025 (lost by 2 votes)
Background
An attorney at a large Manhattan law firm, representing mainly nonprofit organizations on governance, ethics, personnel and compliance matters, and handling workplace investigations and litigation; earlier in his career he was a public defender and clerked for Judge Christopher F. Droney of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit. He and his wife moved to Pelham in 2020 and are raising two young children here; he coaches kindergarten boys soccer and supports Pelham Pride. He ran for the Pelham Manor board in November 2025 and lost to incumbent Tim Case by two votes, 1,222 to 1,220, after a manual recount. He is running again in 2026 alongside Chris Dlutowski on the Democratic and Manor Together lines.
Why they say they’re running
He says the Village can “do more—without taxing more” through continued fiscal responsibility combined with strategic and professional long-term planning. He notes that Pelham Manor’s last comprehensive plan dates to 1979, the oldest of any Westchester village, and argues the incumbent board’s approach to traffic safety shows it is time for change. On flooding, he calls the Highbrook project welcome progress but says the Village’s engineers modeled it to prevent surcharging only through a one-year storm, and only if the blockage under Glover Field is fixed.
Prior public service
No prior elected office. Ran for the Pelham Manor Board of Trustees in 2025.

Town of Pelham Town Supervisor & Town Clerk · 2-year terms
Re-run required by new state law
Context: Mohan defeated Republican Dan McLaughlin 2,115 to 1,456 in 2025. Miller defeated Borsella 2,108 to 1,457. Both races were decided by similar margins. Town Council seats (Burke, McLoughlin, Jenks, Curtin — 4-year terms) are not up in 2026.
A new state law shifts odd-year municipal elections to even years. As a result, Supervisor Mohan and Clerk Miller — both elected in November 2025 — must run again in 2026 to secure full two-year terms.
Democratic Party
Theresa Mohan
Incumbent Town Supervisor · First woman elected to the role · Attorney
Background
Elected Town Supervisor in November 2025, becoming the first woman to hold the position. An attorney by training, she has served as General Counsel and Head of Human Resources for organisations of more than 15,000 people and currently teaches leadership and ethics at Fordham Law School. She previously served three terms as a Village of Pelham trustee, and says that during that tenure she helped secure grants that expanded the village’s sustainability goals without raising taxes. In her October 9 campaign statement she lists what she says the Town has done in her first nine months: modernized the Town website and upgraded internal systems for finance, taxes, communications and administration; strengthened procurement practices, with a commitment to competitively bid significant Town contracts; expanded the Recreation Commission, after the department ended last year with a significant shortfall, and worked to strengthen its financial oversight, improve operations and better understand the relationship between program revenues and expenses; created the Sustainability Advisory Board, which she says is expected to receive its first grant for a Town-wide tree inventory; made the Town’s annual independent audit an integral part of its public governing process; and launched a comprehensive Facilities Master Plan to assess the condition and use of Town-owned buildings and develop a long-term roadmap for them. (Her biography, the reason for the re-run and the first quote below are from her September 21, 2026 re-election announcement in the Examiner.)
Why they say they’re running
She is returning to the ballot less than a year into her term because a change in New York State law moves odd-year municipal elections to even years. In her campaign statement she says that when she took office she heard that many residents were not sure what Town government does, how it differs from the two Villages, or where to go for Town services, and that she believed Town government needed not only to work better but also to be more visible, understandable and accessible to the people it serves.
Prior public service
Town Supervisor, 2025 to present. Village of Pelham Board of Trustees, three terms.
Stated priorities
  • Financial systems modernisation and a more rigorous review of the Town budget
  • Facilities Master Plan: move from planning to implementation
  • A Town-wide strategic planning process covering services and facilities
  • A financially sustainable Recreation Department
  • Stronger procurement: competitive bidding of significant Town contracts
  • Resident access and transparency, including a clearer explanation of how the Town budget works
  • A Community Summit bringing together Pelham’s civic, nonprofit, school, cultural, environmental, youth and service organizations
  • The Sustainability Advisory Board and its expected tree inventory grant
Relevant quotes
Pelham deserves leaders who won’t just maintain what we have, but who will plan boldly for what comes next.
When I took office, I believed Town government needed not only to work better, but also to be more visible, understandable, and accessible to the people it serves.
Rather than simply carrying prior assumptions forward, we are asking where costs can be controlled, where operations can be improved, and where taxpayers are getting — or should be getting — better value.
The first 100 days showed what was possible. The months since have shown that we can turn those early changes into real results that benefit our entire Town.
