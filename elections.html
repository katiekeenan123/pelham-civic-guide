2026 Pelham Elections

Three contested races on the November 3, 2026 ballot. For the first time since 2019, the Village of Pelham Board of Trustees election is competitive — and all six candidates in that race have now published their platforms. Here's who's running and what they stand for — sourced entirely from Pelham Examiner coverage.

Editorial note: Candidate profiles are drawn from Pelham Examiner reporting and candidate statements. This site does not endorse any candidate. All facts sourced from pelhamexaminer.com. Register to vote at elections.ny.gov — registration deadline is typically 25 days before the election.
Village of Pelham Board of Trustees · 3 seats · 2-year terms
First contested race since 2019
Context: Hanan Eldahry, who has served since 2018, is not seeking reelection. The central issue in this race is fiscal management — the village passed a $20.5M budget with a 10.1% increase, overriding the state tax cap for the first time. Three seats are up; top three vote-getters win.
Democratic Party
Russell Solomon
Incumbent · First elected 2020 · Running for third term

Now in his third term, Solomon emphasizes a collaborative approach: "I've made it a point to sit down directly with neighbors, local groups and business owners, understand what they need from their village government, and follow through." Key achievements: championed 25 mph speed limit village-wide, supported hiring of a Traffic Safety Officer, worked on sewer infrastructure (over two miles of pipes repaired). Has served as liaison for pedestrian safety, DPW, and downtown business.

Krystal Howell
Incumbent · First elected 2024

Finishing her first term as trustee, Howell is seeking re-election citing unfinished work ahead. "I am seeking re-election because there is still important work ahead — strengthening infrastructure, making serious headway on storm resiliency, improving public and pedestrian safety, supporting local businesses, enhancing village-wide communication, and ensuring that every resident feels seen, heard, and valued." During her term she supported hiring a Traffic Safety Officer, advocated at county and state levels, and worked with neighboring municipalities on quality-of-life issues. She leads with three core principles: respect, empathy and action.

Kristen Burke
Town Council Member stepping up · General Counsel, Mercy University

Currently serving on the Town of Pelham Town Council (four-year term through 2027) — if elected to the Village board, she will step down. An attorney, Burke serves as General Counsel at Mercy University. Her rationale for running: "One of the reasons I am running for Village Trustee is that currently, while there are three attorneys serving on the Town Board, along with a Town attorney, there are none on the Village board." Her campaign priorities: flood mitigation, rising costs, downtown improvement, pedestrian safety, and sustainability. Motto: "Inserviendo Consummere" — consumed in service.

Neighborhood Party · Republican
Arthur Long
Challenger · Ran in 2019

Attorney at Latham & Watkins. Ran for the board in 2019. Says the village is at a "tipping point" — the current board's "unanimous actions have dangerously stretched the Village's finances." Primary concern: fiscal management and debt levels. In their joint platform statement, Long, Speros and Anzilotti describe the Village's current path as 'unsustainable,' citing debt growth from $4.3M in 2021 to almost $18M now, undisciplined spending (including a $3.655M Municipal Center authorization with $800K for furniture), and lack of transparency on the Picture House RFQ. They pledge to: serve without pay, vote not to exceed the tax cap, create an infrastructure plan, and bring openness to Village decisions.

Town of Pelham Town Supervisor & Town Clerk · 2-year terms
Re-run required by new state law
Context: Mohan defeated Republican Dan McLaughlin 2,115 to 1,456 in 2025. Miller defeated Borsella 2,108 to 1,457. Both races were decided by similar margins. Town Council seats (Burke, McLoughlin, Jenks, Curtin — 4-year terms) are not up in 2026.
A new state law shifts odd-year municipal elections to even years. As a result, Supervisor Mohan and Clerk Miller — both elected in November 2025 — must run again in 2026 to secure full two-year terms.
Democratic Party
Theresa Mohan
Incumbent Town Supervisor · First woman elected to the role · Attorney

Elected Town Supervisor in November 2025, making her the first woman to hold the position. Attorney by training; served as General Counsel and Head of Human Resources for organizations of over 15,000 people; currently teaches leadership and ethics at Fordham Law School. Former Village of Pelham trustee (third term). Campaign theme: "Pelham deserves leaders who won't just maintain what we have, but who will plan boldly for what comes next." During her village tenure, helped secure multiple grants expanding sustainability goals without raising taxes. Running for re-election after less than a year in office due to a New York State law change that requires her to return to the ballot. During her first nine months she has focused on financial systems modernization, facilities planning, and improving resident access to Town government.
