About This Site

Editorial Philosophy & Community Feedback

The Pelham Engagement Project is independent and resident-created. It is not affiliated with any candidate, political party, or governing body. It does not endorse candidates or advocate for particular policy outcomes. It exists to make public information easier to find and understand.

Mission

To increase civic awareness and engagement in local matters in Pelham, NY — by making public information accessible, understandable, and actionable for every resident.

How this site works

Where the information comes from

It depends on the page, and the page says so. Facts, tax figures and the Gov 101 pages are drawn from official government sources — adopted budgets, published tax rates, and the village, town, county and district websites. Current issues are drawn from local news coverage and official meeting records. Nothing here is sourced from rumour, social media, or private conversations. Figures that appear in more than one place are stored once, with their date and source, so the same number cannot drift between pages.

How issues are chosen

Issues are included when a topic has appeared at least twice in public meetings or local coverage, with more than one party involved. Where possible, the two most recent sources are cited.

How candidate information is handled

Drawn from candidates' own statements as published in the Pelham Examiner and other publicly available information — not from independent interviews, because this site does no original reporting. All candidates in each race are presented through the same six-slot template. Where the public record does not fill a slot, the profile says so, so that a short profile reads as a gap in the record rather than as a judgement about the campaign.

How AI is used

Meeting summaries are generated from official recordings and transcripts, then reviewed before publishing. The Ask Pelham assistant answers only from the sources listed below. Both are labelled as AI-generated wherever they appear, and both link to the original recording or document. Names the transcription could not resolve are marked rather than guessed at. Always check the original source before relying on anything here.

How errors are corrected

Anyone can report an error using the form below — no account, no name required. Corrections are logged publicly with the date, so the record of what was wrong stays visible rather than quietly disappearing.

What this site does not do

It does not endorse candidates. It does not take positions on policy questions. It does not replace official sources — for anything that affects your money, your vote, or a legal deadline, check the government's own page. And it is not a newspaper: it does not do original reporting.

Who funds it

Self-funded by Katie Keenan, a Pelham resident. No advertising, no outside funding, no sponsorship from any campaign, party, or governing body.

Sources used by this site

Tax data and government information come primarily from official sources (Westchester County, village and town websites, school district). Current issues and election coverage draw from local news coverage, primarily the Pelham Examiner. Meeting summaries are generated from official public recordings and are independent of any news outlet.

About this project

I grew up in Pelham with a front-row seat to local government — my mom served as a trustee and then Mayor of the Village of Pelham Manor. Civic engagement was part of our family culture.

When I moved back as an adult with my husband and kids, I kept hearing the same thing from friends: local government was hard to follow, information was scattered across different websites and meeting agendas, and it wasn't clear how a resident could actually make a difference.

I spent 15 years at Bloomberg LP building tools that helped financial professionals make sense of complex, fast-moving information. I used that same instinct — combined with AI tools — to build this site. I'm not a reporter or editor, just a Pelham resident who wanted to make it easier for neighbors to stay informed and get involved.

— Katie Keenan
