0:00–0:05Board President's Remarks — Regents, Screen Time, ELA Results & Siwanoy

Board President De Angelis opened the meeting, noted that Trustee Gordon was absent for a work commitment, and had Vice President Marrero restate the executive session motion: the board had met to discuss the employment history of a particular individual. She then took up several issues "in the news and popping up on Facebook."

Regents exams: "Regents exams are not simply going away." Beginning with the class of 2028, passing them will no longer be a separate graduation requirement. Nothing changes this school year and the exams will still be administered. The district has anticipated this for about two years through its New York Inspires preparation, already uses rigorous local performance-based assessments, including some in high school science, and "our high expectations for students are not changing."

Screen time and AI: Elementary technology use averages about 15 minutes per student per day. Draft guidelines are with teachers for review, with opportunities coming for families to ask questions. Student-facing AI is not used at the elementary level at all; at the middle and high schools its use is intentional and teacher-directed.

State ELA results: An unusual statewide decline this year is being looked at more closely. Pelham continues to perform strongly, and its own results will be discussed at an upcoming work session.

Siwanoy playground: The planned fencing will not cut the neighborhood off. The playground stays open after school and on weekends until dusk, then is locked. Bikes are permitted on the blacktop; e-bikes are not; older children are asked to be mindful of younger ones. The proposed 8-foot height makes securing the property during the school day and after dusk meaningful and matches existing fencing. Each elementary property is different: Siwanoy and Colonial have playgrounds very close to the street and neighbors, Prospect Hill has a large land buffer, and Hutchinson has physical drop-offs that require fencing. She encouraged families to bring questions directly to the district.

Mr. Sabalos [VERIFY] and Mr. Mastro [VERIFY] will present the district's screen time guidelines at the October 7 board meeting; elementary principals will share information at open houses; and Mr. Sabalos will hold a parent session at 7 p.m. on October 13. At the secondary level the teacher-directed tool is School AI [VERIFY] (likely the product SchoolAI), a closed system chosen with data privacy in mind.

Evaluations: Dr. Fitzgerald [VERIFY] trained administrators on the Annual Professional Performance Review (APPR) requirements, which is why lead evaluators are on tonight's agenda. Under the current system tenured teachers are evaluated at least twice a year and non-tenured teachers at least three times; the state has given districts an eight-year transition. Her APPR task force is designing a new process focused "more on growth and less on compliance," to be piloted in the second half of this year and submitted to the state in the spring.

College events: The September 15 grade 12 college night featured a Vanderbilt admissions counselor on applications, essays, and early decision versus early action. The annual college fair, co-hosted with Eastchester, was set for September 28 at 7 p.m. in the Middle School gym with more than 70 colleges.

Committees: Dr. Champ and President De Angelis reviewed about 50 applications and seated about 30, a mix of returning and new members; notifications go out in the coming days. The Safety Committee meets Friday and the Personal Growth & Achievement Committee the following week.

0:28–0:34Committee Reports & Consent Agenda 6.1–6.4

There were no committee reports since the last meeting, and with no members of the public present there was no public comment.

Base proportions (6.2): Jim, the Assistant Superintendent, explained that Pelham's tax base is split between homestead (houses) and non-homestead (apartment buildings and commercial) property. The board may base the split on the 2025 or 2026 final assessment roll; the recommendation was 2025 because under 2026 "the homestead property owners would bear slightly greater tax burden." The board makes this selection every year.

Merger teams (6.3–6.4): Girls ice hockey and wrestling continue as mergers with neighboring districts that host the teams, because Pelham does not have enough students to field full teams. Families with questions are pointed to the athletics website or office. Mr. Kastania [VERIFY] is reviewing athletic programs with future growth in mind. Items 6.1–6.4 passed unanimously.
