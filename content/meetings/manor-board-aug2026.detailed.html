0:00–0:07Announcements & Condolences

Trustee Owen-Michaane was absent, travelling out of state; the chair relayed that he remains current on issues and is working with the Village Manager on drainage infrastructure assessment, which performed well through the summer storm and hurricane season. Con Edison removed a hazardous tree after storm damage, with structural repairs that week and aesthetic work the next; no further outages followed.

Condolences were extended for two long-time residents: Frank Barbieri, roughly 30 years in the volunteer fire department reaching the rank of lieutenant and also a member of the American Legion, survived by his wife Diana and sons Chris, Frank and Thomas; and Donna DelPonte, who died after fighting an aggressive cancer, survived by her husband Frank and children Larissa and Frankie.

Deputy Mayor's announcements: The Pedestrian & Traffic Safety Survey remains open on the village website. The textile recycling program runs Saturday pickups, with items left out Friday night and the accepted-items list online. A Siwanoy School student was recognized for breaking another world record.

0:07–0:11Fire & Police Reports — July

Fire (Captain Figueroa, for Chief Greco; Commissioner Trustee Case): 86 total calls, including 4 automatic aid calls — 3 to Pelham, 1 to New Rochelle — and 14 motor vehicle accidents, most on I-95. Twelve in-service inspections and 33 training sessions totalling 161 hours. Storms drove multiple trees-down and wires-down calls; one Pelham automatic aid call was an electrical fire at a business; one bridge strike involved a truck. Firefighters Leonard and Satino Esposito completed their probationary year.

Police (introduced by the Deputy Mayor as Police Commissioner): 352 incidents, including 10 larcenies of which 6 were shoplifting, plus a wallet taken from a shopping cart in the business district. The Chief urged shoppers not to leave bags unattended in carts and to stay off their phones while shopping, and warned residents to guard personal documents and verify unsolicited callers before sharing information.

0:11–0:14E-Bike, E-Scooter & E-Dirt Bike Rules

Responding to parent inquiries, the Chief set out the rules. E-bikes and e-scooters: minimum operator age 16; helmets required for ages 16–17; not permitted on sidewalks; must follow all state vehicle and traffic law including speed limits on roads up to 30 mph and stop signs; must yield to pedestrians. Multiple e-bike-versus-car accidents have been noted, and delivery drivers frequently use them.

E-dirt bikes are classified as off-highway motorcycles under state law and are not legal on public roadways, sidewalks, bike paths or parks unless fully registered and insured. Protective gear is required for riders aged 16–17 on roadways.

0:14–0:17DPW & Treasurer Reports

DPW (Village Manager Luft): 6 street lights repaired, 9 sidewalk sections, 3 tons of blacktop into potholes, and 10 more trees planted. A 66-tree NIPA order was submitted, putting the calendar year on track for about 81 trees. Roughly half of 342 total tons collected was recycled. The Village Hall parking lot fence was replaced. Two grant applications went out — Climate Smart Communities and the Water Quality Improvement Project — and the Manager took on building the website for the Long Island Sound watershed intermunicipal council, which had lapsed for about five years.

Treasurer (combined June and July): total fund balance at the end of July around $19 million, with real property taxes of roughly $15 million tracking the budget forecast and no notable expenditures — a "strong fiscal position" heading into the year.

0:17–0:24Public Hearings — Canal Road Bus Permits

Pelham Parking LLC: Renewal of a special permit roughly ten years old authorizing bus and similar vehicle storage on Canal Road, on a 3-year term with an option to extend and a 3% fee increase, projected annually across the term. Conditions are unchanged and the permitted area is fixed by an exhibit map. Manager Luft noted the permit generates non-property-tax revenue for the village. No public comments were received.

Five Canal Road LLC: A Mount Vernon bus company sought an additional maintenance facility at 5 Canal Road; the board had referred the application to the Planning Board in May 2026, which recommended it favourably as a Type II action under SEQRA with no significant changes. Five conditions were carried into the permit: use limited to a maintenance facility only; the truck route on Pelham Parkway restricted to right turn only, toward Mount Vernon and away from village traffic; all employees parking on site; at most one oversized bus stored on site and two buses on the premises at any time; and no overnight storage of electric buses. Attorney William Null, for the applicant, thanked the village and said they "look forward to being good community members." No other public comments were received.

0:24–0:31NY Athletic Club, State Funds & Budget Amendment

New York Athletic Club, 31 Shore Road: The board approved a site plan amendment and floodplain development permit for demolition of one building and complete reconstruction of two supporting the pool complex, with ADA-compliant and emergency access upgrades, entryway landscaping and drainage improvements. The Planning Board had recommended it favourably subject to a variance, which the Board of Appeals granted. The DEC has emailed confirmation that approval is forthcoming, but the chair and staff confirmed that no construction or building permits will issue until formal DEC approval is in hand.

Snow and ice: A supplemental agreement with NYS DOT brings the village roughly $5,000 more for salting and clearing state roads within the village during a heavier-than-normal season.

Budget amendment: A line-item cleanup for FY2025–26 reflecting actual revenues and expenditures — a practice recommended by the State Comptroller's office ahead of audit — with net zero change to taxpayers and no tax increase. Treasurer Booth was credited for interfacing with the Comptroller's office.

Block party: A street closure was approved for Saturday, September 12, 2026, 11am–10pm, with a September 13 rain date.

0:31–0:37Public Comment — Amtrak on Forest Road

Josh Casper, resident and volunteer firefighter, opened with a tribute to Frank Barbieri, who recruited him into the Pelham Volunteer Fire Company about 30 years ago.

On the Amtrak Forest Road project he raised five concerns: flyers went to only a handful of households on Forest Road and some entry lane addresses rather than all affected residents; work will run 24/7 at some point, requiring track shutdowns; Amtrak's representative could not specify noise thresholds or how residents would measure and report noise, though mitigation was acknowledged; past crews parked along Forest Road and left litter; and the gate to the rail area has been left open during work, which he urged be closed after every vehicle entry given children and "potential bad actors" near the tracks. He asked the board to monitor the project and engage Amtrak directly.

Board and staff response: The village uses the same contact listed on Amtrak's flyers, who has been responsive on trash and related issues, and has passed on the fence concerns; the contractor said it will keep the village in the loop when ready to mobilise. The corridor project runs three years, though not all of it falls in the immediate area. The board settled on two immediate steps — ask the Police Department for extra patrol loops and sweeps, and deploy the village's mobile spotlight along the street, raising awareness without shining it at anyone's house.

Helmet safety: Casper also asked whether the village runs a helmet distribution or grant programme, having seen children riding without them. The board confirmed helmets have been distributed, including at the farmers market, under the "Safety Starts with You" pedestrian and traffic safety initiative, and that officers run assemblies at Prospect Hill and Siwanoy covering rules of the road and helmet fitting.
