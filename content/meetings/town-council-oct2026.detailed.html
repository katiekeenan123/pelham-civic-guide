Opened with the Pledge of Allegiance. Roll call recorded Supervisor Theresa Mohan (chair), Deputy Supervisor Maura Curtin, Kristen Burke, Michael Jenks and Kara McLoughlin [VERIFY] — the last name is garbled on the recording and identified as the remaining member of the Town roster.

0:01–0:03Public Comment — Budget Before Election Day

A resident who said he is running for Town Supervisor noted that the November meeting, where high-level budget discussion usually happens, falls after Election Day, and asked that residents get a high-level budget overview before they vote; he said the budget comes up often when he talks with people. The board answered that those discussions will happen at the October 14 budget session, and that by law the budget has to be filed with the Town Clerk on October 10.

0:03–0:13Public Comment — EMS Station Site Near the Post Office

A long-time resident, speaking for herself and neighbors who came with her, said they had heard the board was reopening the idea of putting the EMS station next to the post office, which they understood had been dropped, and wanted their opposition on the record.

Board response: no decision is being made. A facilities planner presented a few weeks ago and the board is still working out the best location; the current station is not adequate, and there are photos of its parking lot flooding in rain. The options are the same as last year: stay where it is, or acquire a property in town — including the lot next to the post office, if it is available for $50,000 and feasible to build on. A grant for the station was not awarded, but the Town can reapply. The board said it would engage the neighbors before any decision and not surprise them with a meeting, and that the Master Facilities Plan's issues at the Daronco Town House are a higher priority for now.

On the cost figure: the board called it misinformation that it had approved a multi-million-dollar station. The estimate was drafted in May 2024 by Danielle DeVito of Pencil Projects [VERIFY], based on square footage and a previous board's information; neither this board nor the previous one approved it. The figure was first given as $3.6 million and corrected on the record to $2.6 million after a resident raised it.

John Lorsick [VERIFY], who lives near the site, said it is "just a bad location" for an ambulance regardless of price — even free — citing the stop signs, residential streets and the business district's lights around it, and asked the board to convince him otherwise. A second resident agreed that crossing the street to the post office is already hard. A third asked that the time of the October 14 budget session be posted on the website; the board confirmed it is an open meeting.

0:14–0:16EMS Report — September

108 calls in September, with an average response time of about 5.5 to 5.7 minutes, covering both Pelham and Pelham Manor. There was again a day with four to five serious calls in a short period, as in August. The average includes calls established as not life-threatening, where crews do not race; for cardiac arrests, crews typically arrive in under four to five minutes.

0:16–0:17Court Grant & Glover Field — Councilwoman Burke

A resolution on the agenda lets the Town Court apply for a $30,000 J-CAP grant for courtroom equipment.

Glover Field: the inter-municipal agreement with Mount Vernon, both villages and the school board is moving forward. A consultant report is needed before a grant application, and the grant window opens soon, so the parties must decide who pays for it; Mount Vernon thinks the Town should, since the work covers the whole town. The Town's attorney has reviewed the agreement and has no objection beyond the payment question. To be discussed at the October 14 budget session.

0:17–0:20Library Report — Councilman Jenks

Booktemberfest, the previous Saturday: 254 people attended, 30 tickets were sold on the day, over 300 tickets were sold overall, and over 100 raffle tickets were sold for a free Novel Night for two in 2027. Architectural renderings for the library board's transformation project, on display inside the library, drew a lot of interest.

K-Day, September 19: 126 kindergartners signed up for their first library card — 56% of kindergartners in the Pelham schools — along with five adults and five older siblings. 425 people visited in all; the petting zoo's goats and ducks were the favorite.

0:24–0:29Supervisor's Report

The board received the month's general ledger and statement of revenues and expenditures; detail is for the October 14 budget session. Nine months into the administration, the Supervisor said the focus has been financial oversight, modernizing systems, transparency, outside funding and long-term planning.

EMS: the EMS Advisory Committee the board created earlier this year is being appointed tonight, and negotiations with EVAC on its contract, which expires at the end of the year, begin the next day. The goal is high-quality, reliable emergency medical service while examining service needs, operating costs and residents' long-term interests.

Budget season: the Town is pursuing the court grant and outside funding for Master Facilities Plan projects, and reviewing its insurance from a risk-management view, with plans to bid it out so the cost can be built into the budget.

Website and volunteers: the redesigned Town website is live and being refined. Volunteer forms are going online, starting with the Recreation Commission; one for the Board of Assessment Review is being prepared.

Retirement: the Supervisor recognized Ruthan D. Simone ("Rudy") [VERIFY], whose last day is October 31, for her years of service.
