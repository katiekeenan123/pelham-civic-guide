📅 September 14, 2026🏘️ Village of Pelham Manor▶ Watch recording
0:00–0:01Opening & Attendance

Called to order with the Pledge of Allegiance; fire safety exits noted for those attending in person. Trustee Winstead was absent on an out-of-town family matter and Deputy Mayor Bennett on a family commitment. The Mayor noted both remained in contact and that collaboration was continuing. Three members present — enough for a quorum, but not enough to approve the previous meeting's minutes.

0:01–0:029/11 Remembrance — 25th Anniversary

Reported by Trustee Case. American Legion Post 50 and the Town of Pelham held their annual remembrance at 9/11 Memorial Park, off Harman Place, the previous Friday, carrying added weight as the 25th anniversary. The board thanked first responders from both villages who attend in uniform each year.

Paul Satorius, a Pelham Manor Fire Department member, was added to the list of names read at the ceremony, his death having been officially classified as 9/11-related.

0:02–0:05Historic Police Booth & Stormwater

Reported by Trustee Owen-Michaane and the Village Manager. Restoration of the historic police booth at Four Corners is nearly complete. A healthy tree came down on it during a storm several weeks earlier — a setback, and a reminder of the risk the village's tree canopy carries in high winds. Roof shingles were due to arrive the following day, September 15; completion is a scheduling matter from there. Town historian Arthur Senta was thanked for his input on colour and aesthetics.

Stormwater mitigation: the village's continued investment was credited with strong performance through several large rainstorms and flash floods. Nearby communities fared worse — one city up the road saw flooding severe enough that someone had to be rescued from a vehicle.

0:05–0:06In Memoriam

The Mayor announced the deaths of two long-time residents. Tish Davie — a long-time Pelham Manor resident who survived a double lung transplant and continued working at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. Kay Teeley — a 50-year resident, a Siwanoy School mother, and an accomplished professional in the music industry; she was about 86, and is survived by her daughter Nancy Riley. The Mayor encouraged residents to read about both.

0:06–0:07August 17 Minutes — Deferred

Approval of the August 17 minutes was deferred to the next meeting. Trustee Owen-Michaane had been absent from that meeting, which left too few of those present tonight eligible to approve them. The Village Manager confirmed the deferral was acceptable.

0:07–0:10Fire Department Report — August

Reported by Fire Commissioner Case. Two kitchen fires, both contained to the area of origin — the Mayor noted one she had stopped by was already out by the time she arrived. Multiple wires-down and trees-on-limbs calls through the storms. 10 motor vehicle accidents on I-95, which State Police are investigating as suspected insurance fraud: drivers cutting in front of trucks and other vehicles and braking hard to be rear-ended. Residents were advised to stay alert on I-95 and local highways.

Five mutual aid requests, to Pelham, Mount Vernon and Greenville; two automatic aid responses to Pelham. Four minor water conditions, none storm-related — credited to the village's stormwater work, with storm-related call volume described as "almost nil." 168 hours of in-service training and eight building safety inspections.

Paul Satorius: the department attended a flag retirement ceremony at Montfort Academy in Mount Vernon, accepting the flag on behalf of the department and in his memory; members attended the Rising Memorial where his name was added to the wall; his name will appear at the Westchester County Firefighters Memorial in October; and the department escorted a participant on the 25th anniversary "Paul's push" run from the New Rochelle border into the Bronx. The Commissioner also thanked a neighbouring department for automatic aid on residential structure fires — its name did not transcribe clearly and has been left out rather than guessed at.

0:10–0:12DPW Report — August

Reported by the Village Manager. Four street lights and two traffic lights repaired; seven sidewalk sections repaired ahead of the school year; park maintenance conducted; potholes repaired using roughly three tons of blacktop; heavy tree cleanup after a windy, stormy summer. Test pits were dug on Monroe Street ahead of the coming stormwater mitigation project.

Monroe Street goes out to bid by the end of September, with an update expected at the next meeting. ConEd repaving: a meeting was held with ConEd paving crews the same day; crews return by the end of the month to restore pavement around Francis, Esplanade, Sherwood, Suburban, Eli and Manger Circle. The village will send a notification blast once the date is confirmed, timed with the Pelham Jewish Center and ongoing Jewish holidays in mind.

August materials: roughly 310 tons collected, 47% recycled — early in the year but on the village's usual pace.

Vote: motion to approve the Fire and DPW reports as described; moved, seconded, carried unanimously.

0:12–0:15Park Use & Street Closures

Wyman Park — Harvest Festival. Saturday October 17, 2–6pm, rain date Sunday October 18, roughly 75 attending. Carried unanimously.

Shore Park — two concurrent requests, both Saturday October 3. Ms. Mizyan requested 10am–12:30pm for a children's birthday party, about 50 people, rain date October 4; Ms. Davis of Suburban Avenue requested 11am–1pm, no rain date. The board and Manager Luft noted Shore Park is first-come, first-served and that exclusive use is not granted; both requestors were told of the overlap and accepted it. Approved together in a single motion, carried unanimously.

Bonmar Road closure. Ms. Marin requested a block party Sunday September 27, 5–7pm, rain date October 4. Carried unanimously; no seconder was audible on the recording.

Pelham Manor Road closure — Halloween. A resident requested closing Pelham Manor Road from its intersection through to the cul-de-sac for a block party on Saturday October 31, 1:30–4:30pm, rain date November 1. The requestor's name and the cross-street names were marked phonetic in the transcript and are not published here — see the official minutes. The required resident signature threshold was met, though not every household signed, and residents were advised vehicles would be inaccessible during the closure. Carried unanimously.
