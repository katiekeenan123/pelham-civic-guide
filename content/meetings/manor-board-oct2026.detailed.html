0:04–0:11Stormwater Update

Trustee Owen-Michaane [VERIFY] followed up on stormwater remarks from the previous meeting, saying that, contrary to claims made outside the meetings, a great deal of work was done well before and since the village's 2023 drainage infrastructure assessment report, described as the first of its kind in the region. The trustee said the board follows its certified engineers on storm design standards, and that residents have thanked the board after this year's heavy spring and summer rain. Grant-funded projects, the trustee added, take time to start.

Monroe Street. Manager Luft said state contracts can take months. This one needed an additional round of legal review after concerns were raised by a party transcribed as "Palombe Park" [VERIFY], and an agency transcribed as "DLC" [VERIFY] was also involved. The village passed the review, though it added time. The trustee credited Manager Luft with writing the grant: $550,000 toward a project of about $1.1 million.

Esplanade and Ely Avenue. An underground water retention tank designed for a 25-year storm. According to the trustee, the engineers judged that a larger design would start to erode the soil. The village has a $404,000 state grant, about 70% of the cost. Manager Luft confirmed that starting work before the contracts are signed would jeopardize reimbursement.

Glover Field. A grant application is in with Westchester County and under review. The village has also applied to the state Climate Smart Communities program for a feasibility study, including a FEMA BRIC (Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities) benefit-cost analysis, which the county needs before the village can pursue its program. Manager Luft said a feasibility study weighs costs and benefits and defines what a project would involve without a full design. The county planning department's first feedback was that the project might not meet its cost-benefit metric. Manager Luft said this was partly because of the number of homes involved and because the area is in a FEMA flood zone.

For details, residents were directed to Village Hall at 914-738-8820 or pelhammanor.gov. Trustee Winstead had no announcements.

0:25–0:33Public Comment — Pension Costs, Tree Canopy & Fiscal Stress

A resident (named on the recording as Evan Shakane [VERIFY]) asked whether the board had estimated the budget impact of state Tier 6 pension reform. Manager Luft said the Village Treasurer is working on it, since rates were updated as of October 1, and expects to include it in the Treasurer's report at the next meeting. The resident said the change will roughly double municipal pension contributions, from about 3% to 6%, for the roughly two-thirds of public employees statewide in Tier 6. According to the resident, it also lowers the retirement age and service requirements, and that tax-capped governments like the village and school district will feel it. The resident called the village's practice of taxing to the cap a good policy that leaves room for the future. These are the resident's figures; the Treasurer's numbers are due next meeting.

The resident also cited treeequity.org, which the resident said scores Pelham Manor 93, third of 14 southern Westchester municipalities after Scarsdale and New Castle, with the shopping district the lowest-scoring zone. The resident also cited a December 2022 Hunter College study for a Westchester County agency, said to show Pelham Manor lost 0.8% of its tree canopy between 2011 and 2016, against a countywide average of 3.1%. The resident offered to send the study to the board.

A trustee then raised the state's recent fiscal stress scores. Manager Luft said Pelham Manor received no stress designation, with a score she believed was about 3.3 on a scale up to 100, where lower is better. The trustee credited current and past boards' financial stewardship. The Mayor noted that Westchester County is undertaking a new tree study, and Manager Luft said she would share details when they arrive.
