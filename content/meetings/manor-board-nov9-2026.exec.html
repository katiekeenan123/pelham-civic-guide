📅 November 9, 2026 ⏱ 24 min 🏘️ Village of Pelham Manor ▶ Watch recording

A short regular meeting of the Village of Pelham Manor Board of Trustees. The presiding officer and attendance are not identifiable from the recording. The board thanked the Fire and Police Departments for a Halloween with no calls for service, introduced a new Deputy Village Clerk, heard the fire and DPW reports, approved the abstract of vouchers, took public comment from a resident about the voter-registration challenges, and went into executive session on pending litigation.

New stormwater grant. The village was told the week before the meeting that its summer application to the state DEC's Water Quality Improvement Project program was approved, for green infrastructure (stormwater detention) at two more locations, transcribed as Play Avenue and Boards Row [VERIFY]. The grant covers 75% of a total cost of almost $1.3 million, so the village expects about $1 million in reimbursements once the projects are built. Village staff wrote the application.

Monroe Street still waits on the state. The bid documents for the Monroe Street Climate Resiliency Project are ready, but the village cannot go out to bid until the DEC gives clearance, and there is no exact date. Staff said the DEC has raised many questions, including a grant requirement to look at other kinds of bio-retention across the watershed, and was pleased to hear of the new grant, which staff hope will help push the project "over the clearance line." The new grant work may be bid together with it.

New Deputy Village Clerk. Village Manager Luft, the hiring officer for most village positions, received over 60 résumés and held first and second interviews. Darling Sovala [VERIFY], a lifelong Pelham resident with a background in project management and client services, was appointed and took a ceremonial oath at the meeting.

Fire safety. The Fire Department reported fewer calls than in recent months but several large ones, including mutual aid to the Village of Pelham for a lithium-ion battery fire in a garbage truck, and a barge fire on which, the department said, its automatic aid agreement and joint training worked. Lithium-ion battery safety was the theme of school fire-prevention visits for a second year, with over 1,000 safety packets handed out. Residents were urged to use only a device's own charger, not to charge devices in front of a doorway, to take care with deep fryers at Thanksgiving, and not to pile leaves in front of fire hydrants.

Public comment. A resident of 64 years who spoke at the previous meeting about police visits to her family over voter-registration challenges asked the trustee who had been absent then for a response and a condemnation. The trustee said they had no prior knowledge of it, said "I'm sorry it happened to you" and "we have a better use for our police," and hoped the community could "pull together." The resident said she had since learned that both county Board of Elections commissioners had the authority to send investigators rather than police, had a productive conversation with a Board of Elections official, and has retained an attorney.
