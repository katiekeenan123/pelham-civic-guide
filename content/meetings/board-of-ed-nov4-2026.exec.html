📅 November 4, 2026 ⏱ 126 min 🏫 Pelham Union Free School District 👥 Board President De Angelis (chair) · Dr. Champ ▶ Watch recording

The Board of Education's November business meeting, the day after Election Day. The President reminded the community that the board is nonpartisan, gave bond and budget updates, and the district honored 13 National Merit Commended Students, which Dr. Champ said may be the largest group in at least 15 years, one National Merit Semifinalist, and students named to NYSSMA Area All-State and All-State ensembles. Most of the evening went to screen use in kindergarten through second grade: three parents asked the board to remove one-to-one Chromebooks in K–2, and board members and administrators responded at length. Every vote carried.

K–2 Chromebooks. Gina Malik [VERIFY], speaking for a new parent group, the Pelham Learning Alliance, said there is no data that Chromebooks in K–2 are as good as teacher-led learning, pointed to districts including New York City, Croton-Harmon and Bronxville that have pulled back devices in the early grades, and asked for more transparency, citing headphones parents were asked to buy without being told what they were for. John Moynihan [VERIFY], a kindergarten parent, called the youngest students "the guinea pigs of an unregulated ed tech industry," criticized gamified apps, and asked the board to remove one-to-one devices in K–2 now rather than keep debating. Tom Santer [VERIFY], who also spoke in October, cited research on reading from screens and asked, at a minimum, that parents be allowed to opt their children out of devices.

The district's response. Administrators said the district has been researching screen use for about 18 months, is now collecting baseline data from teachers on actual classroom use, and is "not waiting for the state to pass a law." Under current guidelines, kindergarten should use only brief, teacher-guided technology, and grades 1–2 only selective tools for accessibility and language support; teachers have been told that if a screen is just replacing instruction, not to use it. Dr. Comerford [VERIFY] described the next steps, all this school year: feedback sessions with the PTAs, including SEPTA, and with teachers; a "World Café" community discussion; and a district-wide survey of students, teachers and parents. Board members asked parents to say specifically what more "transparency" would mean, and suggested the district say what it is considering and when it will re-evaluate, possibly at the end of the school year.

Acceptable Use Policy (first reading). The revised Policy 7315 adds a ban on using artificial intelligence "to automate or generate academic content without prior authorization," and deletes the clause letting parents opt their children out of computer use. A trustee raised the deletion; administrators said opting out is not in practice possible at the secondary level today, where Google Classroom on a one-to-one Chromebook carries assignments, resources and grades. The policy returns for a second reading.

Bond and budget. Slate for the Prospect Hill roof starts arriving December 7, with major HVAC work at Prospect Hill and Colonial next summer. A new state energy rule requires solar panels on the Siwanoy addition; the district's waiver request was denied, and Assemblywoman Paulin's office committed to securing about $60,000, the unbudgeted cost. Siwanoy bidding awaits state approval, with construction expected to begin in spring, and the Siwanoy Microforest will stay where it is, protected during construction. For the 2027–28 budget, the board asked for several scenarios, one keeping current services and others adding universal pre-K and other programs; a ThoughtExchange survey on community budget priorities is coming.

Also: 77 non-resident students attend on tuition, 48 of them staff children; 24 from the Bronx's "Diplomats Row" bring in just over $500,000 a year from New York City; and the district reopened private-pay admission at the high school, with three students accepted so far. The board accepted $36,022 in donations, including $30,000 from the Pelham Civics for the middle school Extended Academy. The graduation date moves to the evening of the 17th; the month is not stated on the recording [VERIFY].
