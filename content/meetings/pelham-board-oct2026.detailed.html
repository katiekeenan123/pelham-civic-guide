0:03–0:08Trustee Anderson — Leaves, Hazard Mitigation Plan & Events

Fall leaf collection runs October 15–December 15, with crews rotating through the village. Pile leaves neatly, out of the roadway and away from storm drains, hydrants and sidewalks. 0:13–0:19Mayor's Report — State Budget Outlook

Mayor Mullen said the New York Conference of Mayors (NYCOM) policy committees have begun putting together the agenda they will advocate for as the governor's budget takes shape. The Mayor had been on about four calls with mayors and supervisors in Westchester and around the state in two weeks, and said "all of those calls were very angry."

In the Mayor's account, this is the first budget year in which federal cuts are playing out at the state and county level. Cities in particular are seeing spikes in homelessness and food insecurity. Municipalities that chose not to exceed the tax cap last year, drawing on reserves in the hope that health insurance and pension costs would ease, now realize that will not pay off. Grant programs have also fallen through: after the federal bipartisan infrastructure bill of three or four years ago, "suddenly it all stopped," leaving projects teed up but unfunded.

The Mayor described a long push to treat village, town and city budgets the way school budgets are treated under the tax cap, with capital investment calculated separately, and said the current rule is "killing a lot of infrastructure projects." With Governor Hochul outspoken against weakening the cap, the Mayor expects "a very aggressive stance" from NYCOM, including arguing that if the state cares so much about taxes it should apply the cap to its own budget. The Mayor said the fight would likely be less collegial than in recent years.

Asked by a trustee whether NYCOM was waiting to see what the midterm elections bring, the Mayor said that is "in the room" but not really part of the conversation. The Mayor added that Pelham is in some ways better off, because the Village, like the Town, did exceed the tax cap, and so will not go through a budget year in severe fiscal stress.

0:22–0:40Public Comment — Village Parking

A Lot 1 permit holder (named on the recording as Keisha [VERIFY], whose son lives in the Village) raised several parking concerns:
• They have been on the waiting list for about four years to move to a lot closer to the train, and staff have said they cannot tell them their place on it.
• They were told that a new applicant with no permit would get priority over them for the closer lot under the software's rules.
• Lot 1 was not plowed or shoveled at all last winter, and the stairs were unsafe.
• They work nights and feel unsafe walking from the train station to Lot 1 at 1–2 a.m.; restaurant patrons regularly fill the lot, and police tell them to call the Village, which no one answers at that hour.
• A car got a ticket on a night when the Village's online overnight-parking portal was down, although police had given verbal permission to park. They described contesting it in Town court as disorganized.
They suggested high school students needing community service hours could help clear snow.

Mayor Mullen replied that every lot has its own waiting list, and that the Village has to be fair to others waiting, so it can only issue a permit when one opens up. The Mayor said Lot 9 is probably the better location and promised to talk with village staff, adding that staff "are giving you an answer. They're just trying to follow policy." On the priority question, the board noted that no one may hold two permits at once.

The Mayor said parking is tight partly because a parking structure is under construction nearby. It will have 220 spaces, 60 for public use, for shoppers, diners, merchants and overnight parkers. A grant several years ago paid for downtown cameras that feed directly to the police front desk. On tickets, the Mayor explained that the Village handles police and enforcement but the Town runs the court, so once a ticket is written the Village cannot dismiss it, even in error. The speaker said the ticket shows a plate with a missing letter and a car of a different color, and was told to raise that with the Town.

On snow, the Mayor said last winter was exceptionally hard: DPW crews worked around the clock for two weeks, off-street lots are often the last to be cleared, and at one point there was nowhere left to put the snow. "We will do our best."

0:40–0:42Item 9 — Site Plan: Reconstruction

Taken first at the applicant's request. 0:42–0:49Item 8 — Site Plan: 17 Irving Place & Curb Cut Policy

A driveway extension with a new curb cut at 17 Irving Place. A trustee said the project highlights the importance of the Village's planned flood mitigation in the Highbrook and North Pelham watersheds, where neighbors are spending their own money to protect their homes. Another noted that the watershed reaches into New Rochelle, so rain falling anywhere in Pelham Heights ends up on this property. The trustee cited a letter in the Examiner that day describing homes along the corridor, and said hundreds more are served by the arterial lines. Asked about street drainage and pedestrian safety, staff said the new curb cut requires a Cultec stormwater system.

Curb cut policy. One trustee recalled a recent application for a second curb cut that the Planning Board rejected, with one dissent, and said the Planning Board has asked the Board of Trustees to clarify how many curb cuts are allowed in residential districts. "This board has not yet delivered," the trustee said, because the code "is not clear." That trustee supported this application because recurring flooding makes the existing driveway unreliable in heavy rain. Others said the rules should allow flexibility, should put safety (for drivers and pedestrians) first, and might treat busy roads such as the Boulevard near Colonial differently from side streets. The Planning Board had floated requiring a pedestrian safety study. Mayor Mullen said drafting has begun on rules for Chapter 98 (Zoning), with relief through the Zoning Board of Appeals, and asked for volunteers; at least one trustee agreed to help.

Approved 6–0.
