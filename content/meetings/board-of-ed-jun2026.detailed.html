0:00–0:02Call to Order

Opened as the last Business Meeting of the 2025–26 school year, with the Pledge of Allegiance. Trustee Will Treves noted the board had met in executive session beforehand to discuss the employment history of a particular person. Board President Jackie De Angelis was absent; Vice President Natalie Marrero presided at her request, congratulating recent graduates and thanking students, families, teachers, administrators and support staff.

0:02–0:10Superintendent's Year-End Report — Achievement

Return on investment: The district performs at roughly the 95th percentile statewide while spending sits at the 44th percentile — the metric Dr. Champ highlighted for taxpayers. Graduation rate 100% (99th percentile); math and ELA both 92nd percentile; elementary science 98th.

Recognition: AP Honor Roll and U.S. News rankings; Emily Walch named a Regeneron STS Scholar; Finn Mullon ranked 11th in the nation in Speech and Debate; awards for the yearbook and the Sock N' Buskin theater program. 63 students received the Seal of Civic Readiness at graduation, the program's third year.

Strategic "big rocks": Multi-Tiered Systems of Support (MTSS) as the largest districtwide focus; ethical AI leadership, with faculty guidelines rolled out this spring and student AI guidelines due fall 2026; and the New York Inspires initiative — Regents exams remain required under current regulations, with firmer state guidance expected in one to two years.

Curriculum: Earth Science Regents replaced with full NGSS for all 8th graders; OpenSci implementation continues; state-required Financial Literacy and Environmental Literacy to be embedded over the summer; a Local History Initiative under Dr. Shord, funded by a PEF grant and tied to the nation's 250th anniversary, lands in fall 2026.

0:10–0:20Social-Emotional Learning & Belonging

All staff were trained in the CASEL framework this year, treated as an introductory year, with SEL embedded in new teacher orientation and two late-start professional learning mornings. An MTSS SEL committee coordinated by Tracy applied the tier structure to SEL — Tier 1 for roughly 80% of students, Tier 2 for 15%, Tier 3 for 5% — and drafted a potential district SEL plan.

Pilot inconclusive: This spring's curriculum pilot ran only four to five weeks. Dr. Comerford reported many positives but no full consensus, citing the rushed timeline; a vendor meeting was set for the following week and a deeper, longer pilot planned for 2026–27.

Grant ending: The RECOVS grant through BOCES, described as critical this year for coaching and professional learning, is ending; BOCES continues as a fee-based partner while the district looks for replacement grants.

Other: "We Are Pelham" 5th Grade Edition ran at Hutchinson on PEF funding, with a third year covered and district budgeting required after that; Tracy secured a grant for communication boards at each elementary school; the high school's digital device ban, in line with the Governor's new law, produced a definite improvement in building climate; 13% of students qualify for free and reduced lunch, and state-funded universal meals drove an ~85% increase in breakfast participation.

0:20–0:30Facilities, Safety & Finance

Summer work at Colonial and Prospect Hill: Roofing, piping in preparation for HVAC conversion, window caulking replacement and limited asbestos-related work, with second-shift piping during the school year coordinated to stay away from students.

HVAC: The major conversion delivering central air comes Summer 2027, estimated at $15 million for the central air installation, with masonry and window work alongside. Window units remain in the interim.

Siwanoy: Plans go to the State this summer, bidding in fall, construction beginning in spring. The last community session on June 15 drew about 20 attendees with positive feedback.

Security: A $1.3M security camera grant (credited in the meeting to Senator Biaggi) is being implemented with KG&D architects and A Plus Contractors, expanding camera coverage and upgrading access controls. Consultant Tom Drake of Altiris remains full-time; the district hit 100% compliance on required drills and used its reunification protocols in a few real-life situations, debriefing after each.

Finance: Budget approval came in at 75%, up 14 points and a return to traditional levels, achieved within the tax cap but requiring roughly $2M in reductions. The audit found no material weaknesses and no significant deficiencies. Forty policies were addressed this year, with Sidney Burke credited for leading the policy committee.

0:41–0:51Public Comment — Classroom Heat at Colonial

Ashley Mills, 215 Loring, parent of three Colonial students, thanked Dr. Champ for engaging by email and acknowledged the district is in a "tight spot," appreciating "mindfulness of taxpayer dollars" — but asked for an interim solution for classrooms that already have electrical capacity: "the teachers are dripping, the students are dripping… they're in the library, like they're not getting real work done."

A second Colonial parent described the same conditions and asked for interim cooling in time for fall 2026.

Concetta Eudicone, 129 Corlies Avenue, incoming Colonial PTA president, endorsed prioritizing rooms already wired and laid out the building's disadvantages: brick construction, no tree cover, a large asphalt area at the rear, single-pane windows and blaring afternoon sun on the back of the school. She cited a second-grade room off the playground a teacher had called "unbearable" in late August, reported 3–4 teachers flagging heat in a PTA survey, and asked for a solution ahead of September 2026, offering PTA and parent help. She introduced herself as the new point of contact between teachers, parents and administration.

Dr. Champ's response: Top floors already have window units and bottom floors stay cooler; the middle level, especially the front of Colonial, "bakes all day." The constraint is electrical — an AC unit big enough to cool a room draws too many BTUs for existing classroom hookups, and the district spent much of last summer wiring rooms and building frames for the oversized windows. It is now assessing which rooms have capacity and what the rest would cost. The state threshold is 88°F, at which students cannot remain; middle-level Colonial rooms were nearly there Thursday and did not cool overnight, so school was dismissed early Friday — a problem shared across older Westchester buildings that day. Parents at Colonial and Siwanoy offered to donate or fundraise for units, but the capacity assessment has to come first. Full AC is confirmed for Summer 2027, with construction starting summer 2026.

0:52–1:02Committee Reports — Bond, SHPO & Audit

Capital Bond Steering Committee: Proposition 1 work begins this summer; bid results came in favorable, "a nice way to start a project," with vendor contracts being finalized. Asbestos and hazardous material abatement notices were posted at Prospect Hill and Colonial as a pre-notification requirement — no abatement while students are present.

SHPO on Siwanoy: The State Historic Preservation Office advised that windows on the new addition should not match the existing ones, to avoid implying the addition is original. The architect prepared alternatives, but the committee preferred the original matching design out of respect for the roughly 100-year-old building, reasoning that the heavily glazed connector already makes the addition clearly distinguishable. Dr. Champ and the architect will draft the response.

Audit and Finance: The committee met the morning of June 24 with three board members, two community members and the external auditor. Partner Scott Oling began preliminary field work that day, returning in two weeks for the year-end audit. Two new GASB pronouncements affect the MD&A section and require budget-to-actual variance analysis in the annual report. The internal auditor is finishing work on food services, tuition billing and a risk assessment. The claims auditor RFP drew one response, with a recommendation due at the next board meeting.

BAN sale: A $30.5M one-year Bond Anticipation Note sold at a net interest cost of 2.644% against a budgeted 3.25% estimate from Capital Markets Advisors — "very positive results" with "a nice grouping of bidders." Interest falls in the 2026–27 budget cycle. The district still expects a fund balance drawdown at year end.

1:02–1:16Special Education, HR & Facilities Updates

Special education: The department runs 700–800 CSE/IEP meetings a year. The MTSS SEL subcommittee met monthly from October through June, moving from siloed chapters toward an integrated support plan, with tiers, entry/exit criteria and interventions now defined and a screener pilot on the Branching Minds platform continuing. Professional development under the ending grant covered CASEL, Restorative Practices and Youth Mental Health First Aid. Inclusion work was credited across the district, with special education teachers and related service providers called "silent heroes."

Human resources: Most positions for next year are filled, with a handful of partial-year leave replacements still open over the summer — harder to fill because candidates prefer longer terms. The APP Task Force could not find a common June meeting time and resumes in September, targeting State submission in March; the plan requires negotiation with both the teachers and administrator unions. The district completed a full year of paperless attendance.

Facilities (Jim, Assistant Superintendent): A $430,000 budget amendment funded from tax certiorari settlements during the school year; summer transportation for Extended School Year programs; agreements with Educational Data Services for bidding services and Emblem Health for insurance; and a $593,000 tax certiorari reserve fund for Assessment Year 2025, sized to known claims at the prudent lower estimate on the tax attorney's advice.
