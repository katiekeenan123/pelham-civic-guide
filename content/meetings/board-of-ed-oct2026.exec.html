📅 October 7, 2026 ⏱ 188 min 🏫 Pelham Union Free School District 👥 Board President De Angelis (chair) · Trustee Cohen remote ▶ Watch recording

A long business meeting built around three presentations: the district's approach to technology and screen time, the annual audit, and the bond construction projects. The board also directed its delegate, Trustee Cohen, on resolutions coming to the New York State School Boards Association (NYSSBA) annual meeting on October 15. Two parents spoke during public comment, both about technology. Every vote was unanimous.

Technology: "purpose before minutes." Director of Technology John Sabales [VERIFY] and instructional specialist Alicia Del Mastro [VERIFY] presented the district's framework, which judges technology by its purpose rather than setting fixed daily time limits; the district said it knows of no scholarly or medical evidence for fixed limits. K–5 devices stay at school, technology is not used as a reward, and K–2 logins for home platforms are no longer sent home. On AI: K–5 students have no student-facing generative AI; at the middle and high schools Gemini, ChatGPT and Claude are blocked, and the only student AI tool is School AI [VERIFY], used only when a teacher directs it. The district will gather student and teacher feedback later in the year before deciding next steps. A parent session follows on October 13.

Two parents asked for more say. Tom Santo [VERIFY], a Prospect Hill parent, asked for more two-way engagement with families, more data behind decisions, and more detailed policies shared with parents. A second parent questioned a 17-minute average daily screen time figure the parent attributed to the district, saying it leaves out smart boards and classroom video, and asked that de-identified usage data be shared, that parents be able to opt K–2 children out of Chromebook use, and that paper-and-pencil alternatives be offered for technology-based homework. Dr. Champ said she would email Mr. Santo the next day; no other commitments were made.

A clean audit, with cost pressures ahead. The auditors, PKF O'Connor Davies, gave the district an unmodified ("clean") opinion for the year ended June 30, 2026. The district spent about $3.5 million less than its revised budget, and fund balance fell $524,000 rather than the $2.9 million planned. Unassigned fund balance is $3.9 million, 4% of the budget, the state maximum. The board noted that special education, transportation and employee benefit costs are rising faster than inflation.

Bond work. Roof, insulation and steam-to-hot-water heating work is under way at Prospect Hill and Colonial, with the new heating systems and windows due in summer 2027; Prospect Hill's slate roof is waiting on material from a single quarry. The Siwanoy permit is with the State Education Department and described as "extremely close," with bidding this fall and groundbreaking on the addition targeted for spring 2027. The Siwanoy project is on budget.

NYSSBA resolutions. The board directed its delegate to oppose Resolution 20 on nonpartisan school board elections, which members supported in principle but said goes further than current law; oppose Resolutions 18 and 19 on dyslexia screening as unfunded or unclearly written, with openness to supporting 18 if amended to be clearly about funding; and support the school lunch flexibility resolution and keeping the current number of lockdown drills.

The board also accepted $8,000 from the PTAs for additional elementary air conditioning and $5,000 from Class of 2026 parents for an award in memory of a 2025 Pelham Memorial High School graduate who died last year.
