0:03–0:09Trustee Anderson — Electric Landscaping Equipment & Green Zone Certification

The day before the meeting, Trustee Anderson and the Mayor met DPW staff and Dan Maib [VERIFY], director of the American Green Zone Alliance, to look at the village's electric landscaping and maintenance equipment. The transition began around 2021, driven by the board, DPW, Village Hall and the Sustainability Advisory Board, funded by grants and planned with the Alliance and Quiet Communities.

DPW's verdict: staff said they "started off iffy" but now call the equipment "the best thing we've ever done" — easier to use, easier to maintain, and less prone to breaking down. Oil use for landscaping equipment has dropped from roughly a case every other week to about a case a year.

Certification: after walking Wolfs Lane Park, Mr. Maib said the whole Wolfs Lane Park system could qualify for Level 1 Green Zone certification. Level 1 does not ban gas equipment; it makes electric the standard for routine landscape and hardscape maintenance while allowing gas for fall cleanup, storm cleanup and safety needs. He will send the board a certification pledge to consider. Trustee Anderson stressed the practical gains: less noise and local air pollution in parks, better working conditions for DPW staff, and lower fuel and maintenance costs.

The Mayor said the village had deliberately used grants to trial new practices — as with the police department's electric vehicles — in the hope of winning staff buy-in, and that it was a DPW employee who suggested, while discussing the Woodland playground area, "we could do the whole park."

0:20–0:24Site Plan — 64 Lincoln Avenue & 216 Third Avenue

The applicant, Michael Jacomeau [VERIFY], appeared remotely. The board had previously approved both a site plan and a lot line change for these properties; the lot line change is complete and the map is filed with Westchester County land records. The only difference from the approved plan is that the two garages were moved forward to accommodate the deep overhangs of a mansard roof. The applicant said the change complies with zoning and needs no variance, having been checked with the building inspector.

Planning Board conditions: before a building permit is issued, a comprehensive stormwater drainage plan for both lots using on-site solutions such as pervious paving and rain gardens to the maximum extent possible, and a complete final plan set; no disturbance of Lincoln Avenue is expected, but any work needing a permit goes through the Westchester County Department of Public Works and Transportation; the approval binds the applicant and any later owner, and changes need board approval. Approved 6–0.

0:24–0:27Item 9 — Avant 640i Front Loader

The purchase had been debated at the previous work session. Deputy Mayor Carpenter said the board agreed the loader would be valuable for DPW snow removal; the objection was that it was not budgeted and would take a large share of contingency early in the fiscal year.

Since then the Deputy Mayor had spoken with Administrator Scelza, who also serves as Treasurer, and learned of revenue that may come to the village this fiscal year — not yet public, to be shared when the time is right.
