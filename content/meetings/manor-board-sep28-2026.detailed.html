Legislation. Spring Road field and Boston Post Road. She recently walked the area just across the highway near Spring Road field, where the board noted a Town lease dating back about 20 years. She will ask DOT for a study, because buses and commercial trucks now cut through the neighborhood. The Senator agreed to add neighborhood concerns to that effort.

0:15–0:22Stormwater Report

Reported by a trustee not clearly identified on the recording, with Manager Luft. The village had no reported flooding and no pump-outs in the nor'easter, while New York City and some Westchester communities flooded. As a coastal community, Pelham Manor's drains are affected by high tides in Long Island Sound and the Hutchinson River, a main cause of flooding in this storm.

The village's 2023 drainage assessment, one of the first of its kind, sets out an infrastructure plan for every watershed. Projects cited:
• Watershed 1: the $1.6 million Highbrook-Amany [VERIFY] project replaced stormwater structures and installed a pipe more than twice the size of the previous one, with additional drainage added nearby to prepare for further work.
• Watershed 3: the Monroe Street Climate Resiliency Project, backed by a $550,000 state grant the Village Manager secured, is advancing through design and goes out to bid in the next couple of weeks.
• Watershed 5 (Roosevelt and Mount Tom area): improved flow and rehabilitated catch basins on Roosevelt Avenue and other streets, with the New Rochelle DPW coordinating on drainage repairs along the shared boundary.
• Esplanade and Ely Avenue green infrastructure: an underground water retention system, not a pipe, collecting runoff from almost four acres. It is designed for a 25-year storm, the limit the engineers set based on soil percolation at the site. A $404,000 state grant covers most of the $505,000 cost; design begins this winter.

The village continues to coordinate with Senator Fernandez and New York City Parks on downstream conditions near the I-95 culverts. The trustee said all of this work has been funded without taking on debt. They also explained why the village does not simply install "50- or 100-year" pipes: those terms describe the storm a system handles, not the pipe's lifespan, and a larger pipe does nothing if the downstream connections cannot carry the water away.

Training. Officer J. Paul [VERIFY] completed the three-day state DCJS commercial vehicle enforcement course in Sullivan County, run by State Police and DOT, covering inspections, permits and contraband interdiction. Board members noted its value given the commercial traffic through the village.

Electric dirt bikes. Under state law these are off-highway motorcycles, not legal on public roads, sidewalks, bike paths or parks unless registered, insured and street-legal; they may be ridden only on private land with the owner's permission or on designated trails. Lieutenant Leal [VERIFY] prepared a guide to e-bike, e-scooter and electric dirt bike rules, now on the village website.
