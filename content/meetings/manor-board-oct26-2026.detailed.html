0:15–0:23Public Comment — Voter-Registration Challenges

A resident (named on the recording as Lisa McLeary Argento [VERIFY]; address withheld), who said she has lived in Pelham Manor for more than 64 years, spoke as a mother. She said that weeks after the close 2025 mayoral election, two investigators from the Westchester County District Attorney’s Office came to her home about her daughter’s registration. Now her adult son had been challenged in an affidavit signed by Mark Cardwell asserting personal knowledge that he does not live at her address, though she said neither she nor her son has met him. On the strength of that affidavit, she said, the Board of Elections asked local police to investigate, and officers came to her door at about 8:45 a.m. on Friday, October 23. She called the affidavit “insufficient at best and reckless at worst,” said she had heard about 16 families were affected, and worried that the timing, days before an election, could make young voters feel singled out. She asked the board and all the candidates to publicly condemn the challenges, and each board member for their position.

Mayor Lapey, speaking personally and not for the board or village, said she was sorry, urged the resident’s son to vote, and said the county, not the village, runs elections: once the county invokes state election law, the village cannot refuse, and the county may have had the choice of using its own civilian staff instead of armed officers. As a practicing attorney, she said, she was surprised the level of evidence in the affidavit was deemed sufficient, and the family would be within their rights to question the basis for the investigation.

Deputy Mayor Bennett said it had happened to her family too, “second time in a row,” that the residents’ children were within their rights to vote, and, noting she is a candidate in the election, that “we’re better than that.” Another trustee said the same had happened to their family in the last election and called it “a targeted intimidation tactic.”

0:25–0:36Board Discussion & Police Explanation

Trustee Owen-Michaane blamed “a small group of agitators,” said the person who filed the challenges had run for office twice, and said that an inquiry to the county about where else such registration investigations happen suggested this was “a coordinated effort to go after a certain group of people year after year” — “voter intimidation and suppression.”

Police Chief Sanzo [VERIFY] said that the previous week the Chief received a request from the Democratic Deputy Commissioner of the Westchester County Board of Elections to verify whether several registrants live at the addresses on their registration applications. Under state election law, the Chief said, local police must assist with challenged registrations in their jurisdiction, in person at the claimed residence, and turn the information over to the Board of Elections; “we did not have any discretion in the matter.” A village official clarified for the record that the letter was signed by both elections commissioners, who designated that deputy, and said the officers went about the visits “in the most dignified way.” Officials said they took a couple of days to confirm, with an investigator at the State Board of Elections, that compliance was not optional.

A trustee asked Chief Sanzo to report at the next meeting on the number of investigations, how many officers and hours they took, the effect on the department, and whether any urgent calls were affected, calling it “an abuse of public resources” — “not by the police” — targeted at a specific group. It was noted that the police had five days to respond and that day five was the day of the meeting.

A second resident (named on the recording as Cindy Quirking [VERIFY]) said police had come about the resident’s daughter after an affidavit that, the resident said, cited the daughter’s social media; the resident said they did not know the man who signed it, called the episode “disgusting” and blamed the Democratic Party. The board recommended she follow up with the county Board of Elections, explaining that officers asked only a set list of residency questions and did not assess the merits of the challenge. Asked whether the county could have used its own investigators, and whether the village pushed back, Manager Luft said that under Section 5-702 of the election law the county may use a bipartisan team from its own office or call on local police, that the decision is the county’s, and that the village cannot refuse to comply.
