0:00–0:07Opening Remarks & Board Goals for 2026–27

Board goal adopted: "Together the Board and Superintendent will support the continued enhancement of student outcomes as well as deepen community understanding of the value provided by the Pelham Public Schools." Two objectives sit under it — building on the district's culture of educational excellence, and demonstrating the schools' value to the broader community — across three work areas: internal and external communication, finances, and data and student learning. The data strand emphasizes growth over time rather than achievement levels alone, tracking state graduation-requirement changes through NY Inspires and Portrait of a Graduate, and a forward-thinking approach to AI and student screen time.

New board charter: Drafted at the summer retreat around listening to different perspectives, assuming positive intent, arriving prepared, disagreeing respectfully and addressing issues directly. It is not a required document and will be revisited periodically for self-reflection.

U.S. News rankings: The State Education Department did not authorize release of AP participation and performance data to U.S. News, affecting high-performing districts across New York. Pelham's ranking dropped as a result — not, the Board President stressed, because AP participation or performance changed, but because a critical piece of data was missing. She called this year's ranking an inaccurate comparative picture and said the district's focus remains on actual student experiences, growth, opportunities and outcomes.

0:07–0:19Superintendent's Report — Opening of School

Calendar: A two-day administrative retreat beginning August 27 on the theme "Systems of Support for All Learners" — widened from last year's "All Students" to include staff. New Teacher Orientation September 1; Superintendent's Conference Days September 2–3 covering MTSS, ethical and developmentally appropriate instructional technology, and preparing graduates for life after school. Students return September 8, the day after Labor Day: a half day for K–6, phased and staggered starts by grade for middle and high school. Annual board facilities tours begin at the elementary schools to view summer roofing work and end at the high school on Freshman/Parent Orientation Night.

Reporting change: Cabinet members will continue submitting written reports and memos; Dr. Champ will summarize highlights publicly, with follow-up questions directed to individual cabinet members. The approach will be trialed over several meetings.

Staffing: An elementary teacher at Hutchinson was appointed district Mentor Teacher, succeeding Miss Connelly, who moved to Assistant Principal at the Middle School. Resignations included a late Social Studies retirement; a nurse opening and other vacancies were being filled with the goal of being fully staffed for day one. A separate Equipment Manager role was created, one per athletic season — the combined Athletic Scheduler/Equipment Manager model proved unworkable — funded by reallocation within the existing athletics budget, and expected to pay for itself through tighter inventory control.

Enrollment: Elementary rosters opened electronically August 28, with section sizes monitored closely to stay within budgeted allocations.

0:19–0:22Air Conditioning — Ready for Day One

Central air at Colonial, Prospect Hill and Siwanoy remains scheduled as a 2027 capital project. As a stop-gap, portable and window units were installed over the summer — 4 at Prospect Hill, 4 at Siwanoy, 8 at Colonial, the Colonial allocation completing classroom spaces and the nurse's office on the main floor. Basements were excluded as they run 5–10 degrees cooler on hot days.

The PTA Council approved the fundraising process on August 25 and letters were going out to families; the district procured the units up front to secure consistent models, correct BTU ratings and better pricing, with the PTAs reimbursing. Facilities staff Jim and John Condon were credited for delivering. Dr. Champ framed the goal plainly: "This should really position us so that we don't have to close school for heat again" — a reference to the early dismissal that prompted parents to speak at the June 24 meeting.

Repurposing: Once the elementary schools have central air, the window units move to the high school, which was not included in the capital projects for HVAC. "It may not be the most elegant solution, but it's functional."

0:22–0:33Digital Registration, Screen Time & Title IX

Digital registration: Conversion covered the 5th→6th and 8th→9th grade transitions. As of August 25, 88% of incoming 6th grade and 83% of incoming 9th grade applications were received and complete, with 20 or fewer outstanding to be handed to individual buildings at the start of school.

Screen time / EdTech guidelines: Drafted over the summer on an intentional-use framework distinguishing active, educational technology use from passive use. Feedback runs through the administrator retreat and Conference Days, then the Personal Growth & Achievement Committee in early September, with a full board presentation on October 7 and a parent session to follow. Trustee Kathryn Cohen urged proactive outreach to preschool-age families, who in other districts have been the most vocal while having the least familiarity with how technology is actually used in schools. Context cited: screen time rose sharply during the pandemic, New York's grades 3–8 assessments are computer-based, and the district must teach the K–12 State Digital Literacy Standards. A board member noted the earlier AI guidelines parent meeting went unrecorded — "a lesson learned" — and asked that any screen time session be recorded.

Title IX: A public-facing Title IX webpage is being finalized as a single place for policies, procedures, contacts and the required complaint form, alongside an internal employee portal on the Frontline platform.

0:34–0:56Consent Agenda, Finances & Policies

Meeting dates (5.1): Work sessions revert to a 7:00 PM start from 7:30 as a courtesy to staff who attend; the February 10 executive session for 10-year reviews starts at 6:00 PM. The board agreed to avoid adding executive session or business items to work session nights unless necessary.

Board goals (5.5): A member proposed adding explicit advocacy language — "leverage board advocacy opportunities and relationships to advance district priorities at the regional and state levels" — which was accepted, with a further suggestion that advocacy should also rally the community rather than the board alone.

Finances (5.7–5.9): Treasurer's reports for the periods ending May 31 and June 30, 2026 were presented; the overnight interest rate at June 30 was 3.5%, with the market split on direction. A 2025–26 budget transfer was requested in anticipation of a special settlement whose status is unknown — if not expensed the funds flow to fund balance, and if a settlement occurs next year it will be appropriated from fund balance then.

Safety plan (5.10): State law requires an approved district-wide safety plan by September 1. The 30-day public comment period was satisfied and no comments were received.

Policies: Policy 6110, the Code of Ethics, was retained as last adopted on annual review. The 2026–27 Code of Conduct was adopted unchanged after its public reading drew no feedback, to be posted on the district website and included in student handbooks, with an in-depth review targeted at the Policy Committee by roughly April.

Thought Exchange survey: The Superintendent circulated a survey to capture outstanding "parking lot" items, using a tool that generates AI follow-up questions. Five respondents are needed for synthesized results and only three board members had completed it. Members noted some AI-generated follow-ups solicited strategy-level answers outside the board's scope; the Superintendent acknowledged the experimental use.

0:56–1:09Personnel & Advocacy Update

Personnel (7.1–7.4): Six-period assignment overages; contractual stipends spanning curriculum coordinators, scheduling assistants, elementary teachers-in-charge, school culture coaches, team leaders and clubs from drama to yearbooks to STEAM coaching; lunch duty coverage; coaching appointments; substitutes. A leave-replacement library teaching assistant was appointed and a teacher transferred from Hutchinson to Prospect Hill. A new payroll clerk was appointed after the district ran roughly five months without one, with Jim covering payroll in the interim.

Advocacy — Trustee Kathryn Cohen reported on five meetings in a month. Aug 3: a session with the State Department of Public Service on the electric school bus mandate, covering implementation and grid challenges; the region will be notified when the formal public comment period opens, and DPS staff want to hear directly from superintendents about local impact. Aug 11: meetings with Congressman Latimer's office on IDEA funding — a proposed "40 by 40" campaign to raise the federal contribution to the originally authorized 40% by 2040, never fully funded since 1975 — plus career and technical education. Aug 24: a first regional advocacy meeting where EdTech and Universal Pre-K emerged as the top shared concerns. Aug 25: a steering committee meeting and a 30-minute introduction with the Governor's new Deputy Secretary of Education, whose stated interests are literacy standards and the Universal Pre-K rollout; the region raised Universal Pre-K staffing requirements and a request for further guidance on New York Inspires.
