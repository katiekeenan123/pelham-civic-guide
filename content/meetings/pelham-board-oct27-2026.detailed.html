0:00–0:10Call to Order & Trustee Reports

Called to order at 8 p.m. by Mayor Chance Mullen; present were Deputy Mayor Carpenter, Trustees Anderson, Eldahry, Howell, Otondi and Solomon, and Village Administrator Scelza.

Trustee Anderson said sidewalk work is finished, DPW is cleaning catch basins ahead of leaf season and is on its second round of leaf collection; residents should pile leaves on the grass strip, not in the road. DPW is closed on Election Day, Tuesday, November 3, with no garbage or recycling pickup; the revised schedule is on the Village website and the Recycle Coach app. Upcoming: a post-Halloween cleanup on Sunday, November 1, 9–11 a.m., at the elementary schools, train station and Wolfs Lane Park; a Liberty Tree planting and dedication at Wolfs Lane Park at 2 p.m. the same day, for the 250th anniversary of the Revolution; the Pumpkin Smash on Saturday, November 7, 10 a.m.–noon at the food scrap drop-off; and a coat swap at the Pelham Market on November 15.

Trustee Eldahry urged care on the roads at Halloween and reminded residents to vote on November 3. Trustee Howell noted that pedestrian and bicycle accidents rise in the weeks after clocks fall back on November 1, especially from 4 to 7 p.m., and urged reflective gear.

0:10–0:16Fire Department: ISO Class 2 & EMS

The Fire Chief reported 78 incidents in September, including 38 EMS calls, 18 alarms and six vehicle accidents. On October 5 a DPW garbage truck was found smoking from an improperly discarded lithium-ion battery; the battery was isolated with Pelham Manor’s help and the truck was not damaged. The department’s application for a federal SAFER staffing grant was not awarded.

After an audit earlier in the year of water supply, fire department response, communications and risk reduction, the department’s ISO rating improves from Class 3 to Class 2 on November 1. The Chief said the department will notify local insurers and urged residents to call their own insurance companies, since the rating can affect premiums.

Mayor Mullen recalled the time before Pelham had its own ambulance, when firefighters, all of them EMTs, had to wait 40–45 minutes for Empress, and credited the Town Council, former Supervisor Dan McLaughlin and the fire department for the current EMS arrangement. A board member noted that firefighters’ EMT certification dates to the early 1990s.

0:16–0:21Police, Pedestrian Safety, Cable TV & Mayor’s Report

Trustee Solomon reported 411 police calls for service in September, with summonses, including for U-turns and moving violations, on pace to exceed last year. For the pedestrian safety program, Administrator Scelza and Trustee Solomon narrowed three companies to two, which will carry out feasibility studies in November, taking about three weeks, before proposals and next steps. Village meetings are back on cable: FiOS channel 43 and Altice channel 78.

Deputy Mayor Carpenter, “closing the loop” on the board’s late-September vote to buy DPW equipment, said that vote had been based on revenue that was unexpected at budget time but “might” come in, acknowledging the word could have been better chosen, and that the money is item 11 on this agenda. The Deputy Mayor also congratulated the Pelham Memorial High School varsity football team on an undefeated season and its home playoff game Friday at 6:30 p.m.

Mayor Mullen thanked Pelham Together for its October 21 town hall at the Daronco Town House, and announced Coffee and Conversations on November 6 and November 20. No members of the public spoke in public comment.

0:21–0:52Presentation: Sustainable Westchester

Representatives of Sustainable Westchester, the nonprofit whose members include all 45 Westchester municipalities, presented its “Take Charge Westchester” programs for residents and businesses: RateMate, which compares a Con Ed customer’s usage against all available rate plans (free unless it saves money); Grid Rewards, cash back for cutting use at summer peak hours; Community Solar, with savings of up to 5% a month and no rooftop panels, expected to open more widely to Con Ed customers in 2027; a new program to buy renewable energy credits for about $6 a month; home energy upgrades; and a clean-energy workforce program. For businesses and municipalities they described solar advisory services and demand-response payments, citing the New Rochelle school district and a village solar lease, and suggested the Village look at the DPW building.

Board members asked that the presentation go to the Chamber of Commerce and the Sustainability Advisory Board website, suggested short videos for the new cable broadcasts, and proposed a resident outreach campaign with the Sustainability Advisory Board. A Sustainability Advisory Board member described energy savings at a large Bronxville co-op. The Village is recertifying at the Bronze level of Climate Smart Communities and aims for Silver next year; the presenters offered to help, and to bring a partner firm to look at sites for EV charging.

0:52–0:54Local Law No. 4 of 2026 — Native Trees

A public hearing on amending Chapter 93 (“Trees”) to require native plantings applies only to trees the Village plants, not private property. No one spoke; the hearing was closed and the law adopted 7–0.

0:54–0:58Easement Payment & Local Law No. 5 Hearing

Item 11. The Village will grant an easement for an energy project at the northern end of Pelham, at the New Rochelle border. Mayor Mullen said the offer of $160,000 was negotiated up by about 30% to $205,000 by Administrator Scelza and the Village’s legal team; the money was not in the budget and will help the Village reach its surplus target this year. Approved 6–0, Trustee Howell recused.

Item 12. A public hearing on Local Law No. 5 of 2026, a new chapter allowing temporary storm enclosures for eating and drinking establishments, was set for November 10, with Deputy Mayor Carpenter’s amendment spelling out the business zones. Approved 7–0.

0:58–1:01Lobbying Firm for State Flood Mitigation Funding

Item 13. The board retained Greenberg Traurig LLP as government relations counsel, including lobbying, from October 28 through March 31, 2027. Mayor Mullen said the Village has been talking with the governor and its state representatives for months about a state budget request for “a large amount of funding” for the flood mitigation project, and that staff recommended the firm, which also represents Westchester County and was vetted with the state representatives, the governor’s office and the Westchester Municipal Officials Association. The Mayor said the engagement is “solely focused entirely on getting funds for flood mitigation,” with a 30-day termination clause negotiated by Administrator Scelza; the state budget process has several milestones, and “if it’s not going to happen, we’ll end the relationship.” The board needed to act now because Albany’s internal budget process is starting. Administrator Scelza confirmed the cost is in the budget. Approved 7–0.
