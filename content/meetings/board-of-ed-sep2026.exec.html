📅 September 23, 2026 ⏱ 38 min 🏫 Pelham Union Free School District 👥 Board President De Angelis (chair) · Trustee Gordon absent ▶ Watch recording

A short business meeting that opened with Board President De Angelis addressing four topics she said were "in the news and popping up on Facebook": Regents exams, screen time and AI, state ELA results, and the new fencing at Siwanoy. Trustee Gordon was absent for a work commitment. No members of the public attended, and every vote was unanimous.

Regents exams are not going away, but they stop being a separate graduation requirement for students graduating in 2028 and later. Nothing changes this school year, and the exams will still be given as one way to show achievement. The President said the district has been preparing for about two years through its New York Inspires work; Dr. Champ expects state guidance by spring.

Screen time and AI. Elementary students average about 15 minutes of technology a day, and no student-facing AI is used at the elementary level. "None of our students have access to OpenAI at any level," Dr. Champ said; secondary schools use a closed, teacher-directed system, School AI [VERIFY] (likely the product SchoolAI). Draft screen time guidelines are with teachers now and come to the board on October 7, followed by a parent session on October 13 at 7 p.m.

The Siwanoy playground stays open to the neighborhood. The planned 8-foot fence will not block access: the playground remains open after school and on weekends until dusk, then is locked. Bikes are allowed on the blacktop, e-bikes are not. The President said each elementary site needs a different answer — Siwanoy and Colonial playgrounds sit close to the street and neighbors, Prospect Hill has a large buffer, and Hutchinson has drop-offs that require fencing.

The board also recognized senior Finn Mullen [VERIFY], one of two New York students chosen for the 2026 American Legion Boys Nation, and adopted the 2025 final assessment roll for the homestead/non-homestead tax split because it puts a slightly lighter burden on homeowners than the 2026 roll. State ELA results showed an unusual statewide decline; Pelham's own results come to an upcoming work session.
