📅 August 3, 2026▶ Watch recording
0:00–0:00Call to Order & Roll Call

Opened with the Pledge of Allegiance. Roll call recorded Supervisor Theresa Mohan (chair), Maura Curtin, Kristen Burke and Kara McLoughlin. Councilman Michael Jenks delivered the library report later in the meeting.

0:00–0:06Public Comment — Acoustics & ADA Access

Resident Edward Filby, Chester Park: Commended the board for commissioning the facilities study, then urged that an acoustical engineer be engaged for the meeting room. Separately raised the absence of an elevator, citing ADA compliance and mobility needs of both staff and the public, and warned the board it must either commit to never using the second floor or find a way to make it reachable.

Board response: An acoustical engineer will be brought in; the plan includes acoustic boards between the panels, an acoustical back wall and sound-deadening flooring. On the elevator — "not off the table," but the originally proposed location would have cost over $600,000. The preliminary plan is to relocate all public-facing services to the first floor; second-floor use remains undetermined.

0:06–0:09EMS Report

July 2026: 96 total calls — 46 in the Town/Village of Pelham, 39 in Pelham Manor. Average response time 5.4 min; on-scene 16.7 min; transport 12.9 min. Mutual aid: 11 responses out, 9 received.

Finances: Revenues outpacing expenditures; ambulance revenue at 65% of annual budget as of August; fuel expenses up 40% since March. New ambulance expected end of August.

NY State EMS sustainability law: Took effect December 2025, refined by implementing legislation March 2026. Recognizes EMS as an essential public service and requires every county to produce a long-term EMS sustainability plan with municipalities, providers and hospitals. Westchester County will lead that effort — a shift from municipality-by-municipality planning to a countywide model, and one the board expects to bring additional funding to Pelham's program.

0:09–0:10Website Update

The town website overhaul is underway with no firm timeline ("soon-ish"). Action item flagged: do not interrupt Recreation Department fall sign-ups during the transition, and improve the Recreation landing page, which is currently hard to find from the town site.

0:10–0:11Court & Glover Update — Councilwoman Burke

New court clerk Bruno Barbosa started August 3, covering both vehicle & traffic and criminal dockets alongside existing clerks. No court sessions that week, allowing a phased start.

Glover: Another meeting was convened with local municipalities and the school; the town is still waiting on a meeting with the Commissioner from Mount Vernon.

Parking: The facilities study covered the parking area. A $30,000 EV charger grant remains available if spaces are added, though previously identified electrical capacity constraints still apply.

0:11–0:16Library Report — Councilman Jenks

Incidents: EMS responded to a medical emergency at the library in July with a positive outcome; the library thanked Pelham Manor PD and Pelham EMS. Heavy July rain brought water into the basement near IT equipment — no damage, but a close call. The library served as a cooling center during early-July power outages.

July engagement: 5,207 door count, 69 programs, 1,412 attendees.

Summer Reading: 260 children signed up, 37,101 minutes read collectively; 35 ice cream certificates, 41 pizza, 57 cookie. An app goal-reset issue has been fixed; the program is open to adults too.

Puzzle race: The first-ever event sold out. Plans call for 2–4 per year.

Upcoming: Soccer and tennis clinics the weeks of August 10, 17, 24 and 31 (sign-up on the website). Cheerleading and football registration open. After-school registration opens end of August.

Summer concerts: Aug 3 — 8th Avenue Live; Aug 10 — The Truth (and They Don't Bend It); Aug 17 — The Book Ends; Aug 24 — Philip James Orchestra; Aug 31 — Jackie DiMaggio. Sponsors thanked: Rockwells, Meridian and the Gallo Family Trust.

0:20–0:23Supervisor's Report

PelhamTogether intern Kate McGovern was welcomed to the meeting.

Town Clerk relocation: The Town Clerk's office has moved to the first floor of Town Hall, and a phone has been installed at the entrance so visitors can call for assistance rather than climbing two flights of stairs — a change under discussion since January. Supervisor Mohan noted residents previously had to climb two flights to collect a handicap permit, "insurmountable in some cases," and thanked the Deputy Town Clerk for agreeing to relocate.

Hallway lighting: Eight new hallway lights are needed, ~$1,800 total including bulbs, selected with the architects and chosen to suit anticipated future building changes.

Iron Mountain: The Town Clerk and Deputy Town Clerk are reviewing stored records, have identified boxes eligible for destruction, and are pricing the most cost-effective disposal method.

0:23–0:25Resolution 1 — Personnel Handbook

The handbook rewritten in April 2026 carried two errors this resolution corrects. Personal leave: the new handbook never specified a number of days; the prior handbook gave 2, and that entitlement is restored. Sick leave: the new 8-day-with-carryover policy was unpopular with staff, so the town reverts to the prior 12 days with no carryover. Supervisor Mohan described the omission as "an oversight on my part."

Result: approved unanimously.

0:25–0:28Resolution 2 — Financial Software Upgrade

Quotes from alternative vendors ran $20,000–$30,000 and were judged exorbitant. The town instead negotiated added modules and cloud hosting with its current provider. The upgrade brings a budgeting module, cash reconciliation, cleaner financial reporting, monthly budget reporting to departments, better mid-year tracking, and remote access that no longer requires being physically in Town Hall.

Cost: ~$4,000 one-time this year; ongoing annual cost rises from ~$3,000 to ~$7,000.

Result: approved unanimously.
