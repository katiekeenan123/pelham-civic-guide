📅 September 8, 2026 ⏱ 52 min 👥 Mayor Mullen (chair) · all six Trustees present ▶ Watch recording

A full regular meeting chaired by Mayor Chance Mullen with all six trustees and the Village Administrator present, so every recorded vote was 7–0. Two fire department appointments were sworn in, two school crossing guards appointed, and routine resolutions passed without dissent. The substance of the evening was a resident's first-ever appearance at a village meeting.

A resident reported months of recurring vandalism in the Shore Avenue / Pelhamwood area, near the Seventh Street boundary with New Rochelle. She described someone battering on her front door after 9pm on Wednesday August 26 — a neighbour's camera caught a large group of teenagers coming down from Seventh Street on the New Rochelle side — and, on the Sunday before the meeting, returning to find the front door splintered and no longer locking after an Amazon delivery photo showed it ajar. She said neighbours had reported similar incidents for months, questioned whether Pelham and New Rochelle police were coordinating across the jurisdictional line, and pushed back on advice to simply buy a doorbell camera.

Board response: Mayor Mullen said he had not previously heard of a systemic pattern and committed to checking with Police Chief Green [VERIFY], noting prior successful coordination with New Rochelle over fireworks at Fini Park in the same area; extra patrols were floated as the likely next step. Trustee Solomon pressed the point that calls are not the same as filed reports — the department needs a documented record it can act on and share with New Rochelle — and urged the resident and her neighbours to formally report every incident.

Elsewhere: school zone speed cameras cleared their last hurdle, with the Governor signing the home rule legislation around August 13–14 and the law taking effect this month; the village's next step is engaging vendors and residents. Meetings will be live-streamed from roughly the second meeting in October, equipment having been ordered the prior week. A visiting state legislator referenced $300,000 in road funding awaiting a letter before work can start and $500,000 in flood mitigation funding already allocated.
