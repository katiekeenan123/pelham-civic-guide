0:33–0:59Insurance — NYMIR Presentation & Broker Appointment

Representatives of Brown & Brown presented the New York Municipal Insurance Reciprocal (NYMIR) as an alternative to the Town's commercial insurance. The presenters' names are not clear on the recording. Town staff were thanked for gathering the information over about six or seven weeks.

Services: NYMIR insures municipalities only, in New York. Members get a risk-management team that visits and makes recommendations (playgrounds, policies) every year or two; regional and on-site training, including snow and ice and work-zone safety; monthly webinars on required annual trainings (sexual harassment, workplace violence, cybersecurity); an online learning platform with over 400 trainings and over 100 downloadable resources; a computerized maintenance management program for vehicles, buildings and public works assets, which the presenters valued at about $10,000 and said is free to members; and a cybersecurity specialist who runs tabletop exercises. The proposal includes cyber coverage, which the Town does not have now. The presenters also described protections for landowners when land is used for public recreation.

Cost and coverage: the Town has been told its current coverage is going up as much as 16%. The current insurance is procured through Meridian and written by Travelers; the new coverage would be procured through Brown & Brown and written by NYMIR. On flood coverage, the Town buys an extra $500,000 for the Town House for about $20,000, giving it $1.5 million, with $1 million on the other two buildings; the review had found gaps in the Town's current insurance, which a board member said have since been cured except for flood. On liability, the presenters said the current program provides $2 million per occurrence and $4 million aggregate plus $10 million excess, about $12 million and $14 million in total; NYMIR's would be about $12 million per occurrence and $22 million aggregate, roughly matching the per-occurrence limit and about doubling the aggregate. The presenters also discussed negotiating a multi-year arrangement with PERMA [VERIFY], the Town's current carrier for another line of coverage, which is not part of this proposal. Public officials coverage extends to library trustees.

Financial strength: asked about assessments, the presenters said NYMIR, formed in the early 1990s as a reciprocal when commercial insurers were leaving the public-sector market, originally required members to contribute capital, later reduced and then eliminated those contributions, and carries reinsurance above set retentions. They said NYMIR underwrites to break even and has built a surplus, and that its premium increases were 0–2% for many years and about 4–6% a year over the last four or five years.

Decision: because the current insurance expires at the end of November, the choice was to appoint Brown & Brown that night or extend the current policy for a month. Next year's commercial liability insurance had been estimated at about $325,000–$350,000 in the tentative budget. Board members said the switch, on similar limits and lines, is a substantial saving they expect to continue, not a one-time one. One member said the Town's fiduciary duty to the community's resources pointed this way.

Resolution: appoint Brown & Brown as the Town's insurance broker and direct placement of coverage with NYMIR and PERMA [VERIFY] for 2026–27. Before the vote, a board member thanked Mr. Salimini [VERIFY] and Meridian for what they do in Pelham, calling the firm "a local treasure", noted it is opening an office in Fairfield, and said that despite wanting to support local businesses, it came down to the Town's finances and its fiduciary responsibility to taxpayers. Supervisor Mohan called it probably "one of the biggest decisions we've made as a board," thanked Mr. Salimini for his candor, and said, "I hope we can continue to support Meridian in other ways." Moved and seconded; carried.

1:00–1:07Employee Health Insurance Contributions

The Supervisor said the Town has historically paid 100% of health insurance premiums for active full-time employees, for both individual and family coverage. To be more competitive and in line with common practice, the resolution establishes an employee contribution rate for new employees and a schedule for moving current employees to it over three years. It was reviewed with outside counsel; once passed it goes to the New York State Health Insurance Program and can take effect in six months.

Against: one board member said concentrating health care costs on employees, who the member believes generally earn less than the average Pelham income, is not a good approach and is "regressive". With next year's premiums estimated at about $20,000 for individuals and $44,000 for families, the member said employees face what is "basically a pay cut" of $3,000 to $8,000 once fully phased in. By the member's estimate, the cost avoided by Pelham households is about 75 cents a year for each employee on individual coverage, about $15 a year if all of the Town's 18 or so employees are on individual coverage, and about $36 a year if all elect family coverage. "I don't see health care as a benefit, I see it as a right," the member said, arguing that the cost should be spread across the whole community.

For: Supervisor Mohan said the resolution is part of a broader proposal set out in Budget Guide No. 2: to make Town salaries competitive, "because they are not competitive," and to give employees a raise to offset the contributions, in addition to a 3% raise. The Supervisor said it is not meant to hurt current employees, but that the Town's current pay and benefits are not consistent with good practice. Another member said a Department of Labor chart the member had sent the Supervisor shows the contribution rates are in line with national averages, if not on the low end. The dissenting member replied that the Town should not strive to be average; a member who supported the resolution said they would like single-payer health care too, but that is not the reality most residents live in.

Vote: moved and seconded; carried. The individual votes are not clear on the recording. Afterward the Supervisor remarked on it being a first for this board and said robust discussion of such issues is "super important"; whether the vote was unanimous cannot be determined from the recording, so check the Town's minutes for the tally.
