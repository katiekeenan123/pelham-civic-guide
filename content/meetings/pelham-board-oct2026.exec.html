📅 October 13, 2026 ⏱ 53 min 👥 Mayor Mullen (chair) · Trustee Eldahry absent ▶ Watch recording

A regular meeting chaired by Mayor Chance Mullen. Trustee Eldahry was expected late but did not arrive, so every recorded vote was 6–0. Village Administrator Scelza attended. The evening covered trustee and administrator updates, the Mayor's read on a difficult state budget season ahead, a long public comment on village parking, two site plans, and new building-department software. There was no executive session.

The Mayor expects a hard state budget fight. Mayor Mullen said the New York Conference of Mayors (NYCOM) is assembling its legislative agenda, and that calls with mayors and supervisors across Westchester and the state over the past two weeks had been "very angry." In the Mayor's account, federal cuts are now reaching state and county budgets, grant programs have fallen through and left infrastructure projects stranded, and health insurance and pension costs are rising. Municipalities that drew on reserves last year rather than exceed the tax cap are now in a hard spot. The Mayor described a long-running push to count capital spending outside the tax cap, as it already is for school budgets, and expects NYCOM to take "a very aggressive stance" this year, including arguing that the state should apply the tax cap to its own budget, despite Governor Hochul's opposition to weakening the cap. The Mayor also said the Village, like the Town, is better placed because it did exceed the cap.

Parking took up most of public comment. A Lot 1 permit holder, who works nights, said they have been on the waiting list for about four years to move to a closer lot and cannot learn their place on it. They said Lot 1 was never plowed last winter, restaurant patrons regularly fill the lot, and walking from the train station at 1–2 a.m. feels unsafe. They also described a ticket issued on a night the online parking portal was down, despite verbal permission from police. In reply, Mayor Mullen said every lot has its own waiting list and the Village has to be fair to everyone on it, but promised to talk with village staff about a move to Lot 9. The Mayor said a parking structure under construction downtown will have 220 spaces, 60 of them for public use (shoppers, diners, merchants and overnight parkers). Downtown cameras feed directly to the police front desk. The Village cannot dismiss a ticket once it is written, because the Town runs the court. On snow, the Mayor said DPW crews worked around the clock for two weeks last winter and at one point had nowhere left to put the snow.

Curb cut rules are overdue. Approving a driveway extension and curb cut at 17 Irving Place, where recurring flooding from the Highbrook watershed makes the existing driveway unreliable in heavy rain, trustees acknowledged that the Planning Board has asked the Board to clarify how many curb cuts are allowed in residential districts, and the code is unclear. One trustee said the Board "has not yet delivered" an answer. The Mayor has begun drafting rules to go in Chapter 98 (Zoning), with relief through the Zoning Board of Appeals, and asked for trustee volunteers. Safety, for drivers and pedestrians, was named as the guiding principle.

Also: the Village is returning to public access television, with new broadcast equipment installed the day of the meeting and the October 27 meeting planned to air live (Trustee Solomon). The Board approved GovWell Technologies software to rebuild the building department and land use application system. Fall leaf collection runs October 15–December 15 (Trustee Anderson). Overnight Metro-North bridge work runs 10 p.m.–5 a.m. from October 19 through October 27. Applications for the county firefighter exam are due November 2.
