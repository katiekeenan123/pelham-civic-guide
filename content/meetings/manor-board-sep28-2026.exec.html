📅 September 28, 2026 ⏱ 36 min 🏘️ Village of Pelham Manor 👥 Mayor Lapey (chair) · full board present ▶ Watch recording

A regular meeting chaired by Mayor Lapey with all four trustees present. The Village Attorney, tied up with trial-ready cases, joined only for the executive session. State Senator Fernandez opened the evening with an update for the village, and the rest of the agenda moved quickly through reports, a street closure, two financial resolutions and vouchers. Every vote carried unanimously and no member of the public spoke.

The village came through the recent nor'easter with no reported flooding and no pump-outs. Surrounding communities flooded. The board credited catch basin cleaning ahead of the storm and years of stormwater work guided by the village's 2023 drainage assessment, all paid for without borrowing. Recent and coming projects include the $1.6 million Highbrook-Amany [VERIFY] project, which installed a pipe more than twice the size of the old one; the Monroe Street Climate Resiliency Project, backed by a $550,000 state grant and going out to bid in the next couple of weeks; and a green infrastructure project under Esplanade and Ely Avenue, with a $404,000 state grant toward its $505,000 cost and design starting this winter.

Senator Fernandez reported on several issues along the village's borders. She is still mediating with Mount Vernon over noise from events near the border and has bought her own decibel meter to document it. Catch basins on the Pelham Manor–Bronx border are being repaired and emptied with a grant the Village Manager secured. State money she allocated will go to ADA upgrades and police locker rooms at Village Hall. Her Pelham Picture House funding now sponsors free senior movies every Tuesday, September through June. She also agreed to back the village's request that the state DOT de-designate Boston Post Road as an access highway for oversized vehicles.

Police: a suspect in the theft of an Aston Martin the day before Thanksgiving 2025 was arrested the Thursday before the meeting, after almost a year on the run. Officer Brandon Lustig [VERIFY] broke his hand arresting a shoplifting suspect who fled ShopRite, and is expected back on patrol in about two months. The department reminded residents that electric dirt bikes are not legal on village roads, sidewalks or parks unless fully registered, insured and street-legal; a guide is on the village website. A six-hour AAA defensive driving course, which qualifies for insurance and license point reductions, runs at Village Hall on Saturday, November 21 from 9 a.m.

The October meeting moves to Tuesday, October 13, because Monday the 12th is Columbus Day and Indigenous Peoples' Day.
