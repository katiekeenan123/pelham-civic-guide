📅 October 13, 2026 ⏱ 36 min 🏘️ Village of Pelham Manor 👥 Mayor Lapey (chair) · full board present ▶ Watch recording

A short regular meeting, held on Tuesday because Monday was Columbus Day and Indigenous Peoples' Day. Mayor Lapey chaired with all four trustees present. The agenda covered trustee updates on road safety and stormwater, a proclamation for a local student, fire and DPW reports, vouchers, and two residents' questions. Every vote carried unanimously, and the board went into executive session on pending litigation.

Trustee Owen-Michaane [VERIFY] said the village's stormwater projects are moving but cannot start construction until the state contracts are signed. Work begun before then would not be reimbursed. The Monroe Street project has a $550,000 state grant toward a cost of about $1.1 million. Village Manager Lindsey Luft said the state contract needed an extra round of legal review because concerns had been raised by a party transcribed as "Palombe Park" [VERIFY]. The village passed that review, and Manager Luft said the bid would be published the week of the meeting. The Esplanade and Ely Avenue project, an underground retention tank sized for a 25-year storm, has a $404,000 state grant covering about 70% of its cost. For Glover Field, the village has a grant application in with Westchester County and has applied to the state's Climate Smart Communities program for a feasibility study with a FEMA BRIC benefit-cost analysis. Manager Luft said the county needs that study before it will consider funding. The county planning department's first view was that the project might not meet its cost-benefit test, partly because the area is in a FEMA flood zone.

The village-wide roadway safety study is due in December. Deputy Mayor Bennett [VERIFY] said the online survey and walking audits are complete and the engineers are analyzing the data. Manager Luft told a resident that no date within December has been set. The AAA defensive driving class runs at Village Hall on November 21. The form is on pelhammanor.gov and goes back to Village Hall, and spots are filling quickly.

Public comment. A resident asked whether the board had estimated the budget impact of state Tier 6 pension changes. The resident said municipal pension contributions will nearly double and that tax-capped villages will feel the pressure. Manager Luft said the Village Treasurer is working on updated rates as of October 1 and expects to report at the next meeting. The same resident cited tree-canopy data said to show Pelham Manor among the best-canopied municipalities in southern Westchester. Asked about the state's recent fiscal stress scores, Manager Luft said Pelham Manor received no stress designation, with a score she believed was about 3.3 out of 100, where lower is better. A second resident said Shore Road could not be used because of construction near the Bronx line, tied to a bridge replacement project. They were concerned about a community event that Saturday running from the Beach Club [VERIFY] to Bartow-Pell Mansion. Manager Luft said she would get the details and send out a community notification.

Also reported: Trustee Case marked Fire Prevention Week, whose theme this year was the safe charging of devices. Case also listed this week's events marking the 250th anniversary of the Battle of Pell's Point, including a reenactment at Orchard Beach on Sunday at 10 a.m. The board presented a proclamation to Arya Galba [VERIFY], who represented the village at the American Legion's Girls' State program. The fire department is running lithium-ion battery safety visits at local schools. Curbside leaf collection starts this week. Gas leaf blowers are allowed Tuesday–Friday 8 a.m.–5:30 p.m. and Saturday noon–4 p.m., and never on Sunday or Monday.
