📅 October 27, 2026 ⏱ 64 min 👥 Mayor Mullen (chair) · all six Trustees present ▶ Watch recording

A regular meeting chaired by Mayor Chance Mullen with all six trustees present, and the first back on cable TV (FiOS 43, Altice 78). Every vote carried, most 7–0. No members of the public spoke in public comment.

A lobbying firm to seek state money for flood mitigation. The board voted 7–0 to retain Greenberg Traurig LLP, the firm that represents Westchester County, as government relations counsel from October 28 through March 31, 2027. Mayor Mullen said the Village has spent months talking with the governor and state representatives about a state budget request for “a large amount of funding” for its flood mitigation project, and that the engagement is limited solely to that, with a 30-day termination clause negotiated by Administrator Scelza. The Mayor said the board needed to act now because the state’s internal budget process is starting, and that if the effort is not paying off by the budget milestones the Village will end the relationship. The cost is in the budget.

$205,000 easement payment. The board approved, 6–0 with Trustee Howell recused, an easement for an energy project at the northern Pelham–New Rochelle border. The Mayor said the offer of $160,000 was negotiated up about 30% to $205,000, revenue not in the budget that will help the Village reach a surplus this year. Deputy Mayor Carpenter said this is the revenue the board’s September vote to buy DPW equipment had been premised on.

Better fire insurance rating. The Fire Chief reported that the department’s ISO rating improves from Class 3 to Class 2 on November 1. The department will notify local insurers, and residents were urged to call their own insurance companies, since the rating can affect premiums. The department’s application for a federal SAFER staffing grant was not awarded. Mayor Mullen recalled the time before Pelham had its own ambulance, when calls waited 40–45 minutes for Empress, and credited the Town and the fire department for the current EMS setup.

Other votes and updates: Local Law No. 4 of 2026, requiring native trees in the Village’s own plantings, was adopted 7–0 after a public hearing with no speakers. A public hearing on Local Law No. 5, allowing temporary storm enclosures for restaurants and bars in the business zones, is set for November 10. Trustee Solomon said two firms will do feasibility studies for the pedestrian safety program in November. Sustainable Westchester presented energy-saving programs for residents and businesses. There is no garbage or recycling pickup on Election Day, November 3.
