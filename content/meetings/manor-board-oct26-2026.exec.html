📅 October 26, 2026 ⏱ 46 min 🏘️ Village of Pelham Manor 👥 Mayor Lapey (chair) · Trustee Winstead absent (ill) ▶ Watch recording

A regular meeting chaired by Mayor Lapey; Trustee Winstead had told the board she was ill. Routine business passed unanimously in the first quarter of an hour. Most of the meeting was public comment about police visits to residents whose voter registrations had been challenged in affidavits filed with the Westchester County Board of Elections.

Why police came to residents’ doors. Police Chief Sanzo [VERIFY] said the department received a request from the county Board of Elections, in a letter that village officials said was signed by both elections commissioners, to verify whether several registrants live at the addresses on their registrations. Under state election law, the Chief said, local police must assist, in person at the claimed residence, and report back to the Board of Elections; the department “did not have any discretion.” Officials said they waited a couple of days and confirmed with an investigator at the State Board of Elections that compliance was required. Village Manager Luft said that under Section 5-702 the county could instead have used its own bipartisan staff, but the choice is the county’s alone. Officers asked only a set list of residency questions; the board directed affected residents to the county Board of Elections.

Residents described the visits. A resident said officers came to her home at about 8:45 a.m. on Friday, October 23, about her adult son, after an affidavit signed by Mark Cardwell claimed personal knowledge that he did not live there. She said she had heard about 16 families were affected, worried about the effect on young voters days before the election, and asked the board and all candidates to publicly condemn the challenges. Other speakers said they or family members had been visited; one said that every person investigated was an independent, unaffiliated or Republican voter, and that one of those visited was a recently widowed woman asked about her late husband’s registration.

Board members’ responses. Speaking personally rather than for the board, Mayor Lapey urged the resident’s son to vote and said, as a practicing attorney, she was surprised the evidence in the affidavit was deemed sufficient. Deputy Mayor Bennett, a candidate in the election, said it had happened to her family too, for a second time. Trustee Owen-Michaane called it “a coordinated effort to go after a certain group of people year after year” and “voter intimidation and suppression.” Another trustee asked Chief Sanzo to report at the next meeting on the number of investigations, officers and hours involved, the effect on the department, and whether any urgent calls were affected. A resident said Trustee Winstead’s absence was her fifth in 20 meetings; a board member noted for the record that she had said she was unwell.

Also: Trustee Owen-Michaane said the County Executive agreed with the village’s approach of smaller, $1–5 million drainage projects, and that the village is applying to the county and state for a feasibility study of the Glover Field area, where several jurisdictions’ sanitary sewers run, to test it against FEMA’s BRIC cost-benefit rules. The fund balance stood at about $14.5 million at the end of September, and the largest revenue item was a $150,000 state grant reimbursement for catch basin and street-cleaning equipment. Manager Luft said pensions are about 13% of spending, up roughly 12% from last year, with an estimate of the Tier 6 changes expected in January. Registration for the free AAA defensive driving course on November 21 closes Friday, October 30 (pelhammanor.gov).
