0:00–0:02Call to Order & Roll Call

Roll call confirmed a full board: Mayor Mullen, Deputy Mayor Carpenter, and Trustees Anderson, Eldahry, Howell, Otondi and Solomon, with Administrator Scelza also present. Every recorded vote that followed was therefore 7–0.

0:02–0:08Fire Department Appointments & Promotion

Resolution 8 appointed a new firefighter, passing 7–0. Matthew DiNapoli was sworn in and began the 21-week fire academy the same week; Trustee Howell, who sat on the interview panel, said he stood out among the candidates. Firefighter Camardela was promoted to Lieutenant and sworn in, with first-line supervisor training starting September 14.

The Mayor noted the Pelham Fire Department has existed since 1893. The board commended the chief, the captain and the department's administrative staff for an interview process aimed at rebuilding with experienced personnel.

0:08–0:13Trustee Anderson — DPW Summer Work & Sustainability

DPW summer totals: 2,772 tons of asphalt placed through the annual paving program; 120 cubic yards of concrete poured for new sidewalks; three catch basins and four roadway sinkholes repaired; 16 tons of street sweeping debris removed; 120 gallons of traffic paint applied to crosswalks and markings. Thanks were extended to the DPW crew by name.

Coming up: a Chamber of Commerce community meetup on livability and sustainability, September 14, 6–8pm at Cantina Lobos (RSVP through the Chamber); the Sustainability Advisory Board at the farmers market on Sunday September 27, with compost collection, textile recycling, free native plants from EcoPell, and information on GridRewards, solar through Sustainable Westchester, and pollinator-friendly yards; and the Hutchinson River Restoration Project "Sanford Sweep" at Glover Field on October 4, 9am–1pm, open to all for any length of time. The Junior League of Pelham was thanked for tending the pollinator garden in Woodland Park.

0:13–0:18Trustee Reports — Schools, Fire, Pedestrian Safety

Trustee Eldahry noted the first day of school for the Pelham Union Free School District and urged drivers to allow extra time, put phones down, and watch for children walking and biking.

Trustee Howell reported 66 fire department calls in August, roughly a third of them EMS — underlining why firefighters being trained EMTs matters for response times — plus mutual aid to Pelham Manor and Greenville. A fire lieutenant has begun upgrading permit inspections for commercial apartments in buildings with three or more families; the name did not transcribe clearly and is left out here. The 9/11 Memorial Ceremony was set for Friday at 8:30am at the town monument across from the train station.

Also flagged: a chili cook-off hosted by Professional Firefighters Local 2213 on October 3 at the fire department, $10 per person; the final Council on the Arts Sunset Series event on September 26; and a newly hired traffic officer. A trustee described watching three cars run a red light at Lincoln and Third near a school that morning — "phones down, heads up."

0:18–0:22Trustee Solomon — Police, Speed Cameras, Live Streaming

Police: 362 calls for service in August. A new officer, a transfer from the NYPD, started August 31 and is in field training. Trustee Solomon thanked the Mayor for his community letter on pedestrian safety and reminded residents of the 25 mph village limit and 15 mph in school zones.

School zone speed cameras: the Governor signed the home rule legislation around August 13–14 and the law takes effect this month. Next steps are engaging vendors and stakeholders, residents included, before installation. State Senator Fernandez — present at the meeting — and Assemblywoman Paulin were thanked for their partnership.

Live streaming: equipment was ordered the prior week with roughly a month's lead time, so the village expects to go live on public access at about the second meeting in October. It is compatible with the new municipal center and supports multi-channel communication.

0:22–0:28Deputy Mayor, Administrator & Mayor's Report

Deputy Mayor Carpenter flagged a Westchester County Office of Economic Development survey for local businesses, live at WestchesterCatalyst.com, suggesting it may precede a second round of downtown improvement grants of the kind the village has won before, and urged Chamber outreach.

Administrator Scelza reported village-wide catch basin cleaning underway, with basins around the schools done the previous week ahead of the school year; the remainder waits on the contractor finishing in another municipality.

Mayor's report: county economic initiatives around business attraction and talent retention, including housing, with outreach to the Westchester Municipal Officials Association and a county "Blueprint 26" effort with the Westchester County Association. The 9/11 ceremony begins at 8:30am Friday, with an 8:15 arrival advised for traffic.

0:28–0:34Public Comment — Recurring Vandalism on Shore Avenue & Pelhamwood

A resident (named on the recording as Catherine Delayva [VERIFY]) spoke for the first time at a village meeting. Her parents lived in Pelham for 55 years and she is caring for their home near the Seventh Street boundary with New Rochelle while living in the city. She opened by praising the police and the EMTs who attended her parents over the years.

Wednesday August 26, after 9pm: sudden, aggressive, repeated battering on her front door. She shouted that she was calling the police and saw a figure run. Police responded; a neighbour's camera showed a large group of teenagers coming down from Seventh Street on the New Rochelle side. Talking to neighbours afterwards, she found they had been experiencing similar incidents for months.

The Sunday before the meeting: she left the house at 5:20pm and returned to the city. At about 9:30pm an Amazon delivery photo showed her front door open. She called police, who reported the house secure; arriving at about 10:10pm she found the back of the front door completely splintered and no longer locking. Small door alarms appear to have deterred whoever it was.

She said the Seventh and Eighth Street area straddles the municipal line and worried it gets overlooked as a result, questioned how much Pelham and New Rochelle police coordinate, and said a doorbell camera alone would not address the underlying problem. She offered to approach New Rochelle PD herself if given guidance on how.

0:34–0:40Board Response — Reports, Patrols and Coordination

Mayor Mullen: "I haven't heard that there's a more systemic thing happening. So I want to check in with the chief on that." He referenced earlier coordination between Pelham and New Rochelle police over fireworks complaints at Fini Park in the same Seventh/Eighth Street area, indicated extra patrols were the likely next step, and recommended a Ring spotlight camera alongside a doorbell camera. A trustee noted the village's own doorbell camera footage had previously led to an arrest.

Trustee Solomon drew the distinction that mattered most: a phone call is not necessarily an official report. He urged the resident and her neighbours to file formal reports for every incident so the department holds a documented record it can act on and share with New Rochelle, noting a broken-in door should generate one automatically. The resident said she would make documenting incidents a priority for herself and her neighbours.

Residents with similar concerns can reach the Village at info@pelhamny.gov.

0:40–0:44State Legislative Remarks

State Senator Fernandez and a visiting assemblymember addressed the board, thanking it for the partnership and commending its attentiveness to residents' concerns. The Albany session runs January to June, so more village meetings are possible in the autumn. A June town hall at the Pelham Picture House on the budget was referenced, and the legislator said they would attend the 9/11 ceremony, which they alternate annually between the Bronx and Pelham.

Funding: $300,000 in road funding has been allocated, with work awaiting a letter before it can begin, and $500,000 in flood mitigation funding is already allocated. A senior advisor was introduced as the local point of contact when the legislator is not present.
