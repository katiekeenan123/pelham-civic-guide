0:01–0:04Defensive Driving, Stormwater & Leaves

Defensive driving: a trustee reminded residents that AAA will give its six-hour defensive driving class at Village Hall on November 21, part of the village's "Road Safety Starts With You" campaign. It can reduce insurance costs and points on a license.

Stormwater: a trustee said the village's stormwater improvements had paid off again in a recent storm. Asked about the next project, staff said the village is waiting for DEC clearance to go out to bid, with no exact date, and that a newly received grant (see the DPW report) may make sense to bid together with it. Asked what the engineers had recommended for the Highbrook project, a trustee said the village doubled the size of the pipes, replaced a number of manholes and did lining work along the street, designed to a one-year storm under current conditions; how far a storm design can go depends on where the water comes from and where it goes, as recommended by an engineer.

Leaves: a trustee asked residents and their landscapers not to pile leaves in front of fire hydrants, so the Fire Department can reach them quickly.

0:09–0:13Fire Department Report

Fewer calls than in the last few months, but several big ones: battery fires, a large fire near I-95 on which the department helped the FDNY, a brush fire and a motor vehicle accident. There were five mutual aid calls to the Village of Pelham, one of them a lithium-ion battery fire in a garbage truck from a battery that had been thrown away, and three automatic aid calls with a neighboring department on I-95 [VERIFY], plus a barge fire on which, the department said, its automatic aid agreement and joint training worked.

Fire prevention: the department visited the elementary schools and other local schools and handed out over 1,000 safety packets; for the second year the theme was lithium-ion battery safety, because those fires are rising. Ten building safety inspections were conducted. With Thanksgiving a few weeks away, residents were reminded to cook safely, especially with deep fryers.

Lithium-ion batteries: a trustee repeated the warning, noting fire after fire in New York City. Most people carry two or three such batteries, in laptops, phones and watches. Use the manufacturer's charger, since others can overcharge; be careful where devices are stored and charged, and never charge one in front of a doorway; and be careful when throwing devices away, since people may not realize what they are discarding, as with the garbage truck fire. The fire section of the village website has a page on lithium battery safety.

0:13–0:18DPW Report — New Grant & Monroe Street

Grant award: the week before, the village learned that its application, submitted that summer, to the DEC's Water Quality Improvement Project program was approved, for green infrastructure (stormwater detention basins) at two more locations, transcribed as Play Avenue and Boards Row [VERIFY]. It is a 75/25 grant on a total project cost of almost $1.3 million, so the village will receive about $1 million in reimbursements once the projects are built. The staff member giving the report confirmed writing the application.

Other October work: a renewed pipeline-assessment certification; a meeting of the Long Island Sound watershed group at which Westchester County Planning presented its Westchester Waterways GIS portal for stormwater laws and resident concerns (linked in the agenda packet); and the county's Coastal Long Island Sound Basin rapid stream assessment program, which monitors whether streams and waterways are blocked and how vegetation around them is maintained, using municipal staff or volunteer groups. Staff made sure a waterway transcribed as "the Bridal Path" [VERIFY] could be included even though it is outside Westchester County. Asked about the New York City side, staff said the city works through its own capital planning and there are no firm timelines. The municipality where a culvert or pipe sits remains responsible for it.

Monroe Street Climate Resiliency Project: the bid documents are being finalized. The DEC has had many questions, including a requirement in the grant agreement that the village look at other types of bio-retention throughout the watershed; staff said the DEC was very pleased to hear of the new grant because the two "go hand in hand," and hope it pushes the project "over the clearance line to go out to bid."

Also: sidewalk slabs replaced, street trees planted, and three street lights replaced.

0:19–0:23Public Comment — Voter-Registration Challenges

A resident (Lisa Argento, as she was named at the October 26 meeting; her surname is unclear on this recording [VERIFY]; address withheld) addressed the trustee who had been absent from the previous meeting. She asked for that trustee's reaction to "neighbors targeting neighbors," with local police diverted to question families about voter residency, which she said was based on hearsay and "zero backup evidence," and asked for a condemnation. As a Pelham Manor resident of 64 years, she said, it was very jarring; it had happened to her family twice, and with two more children at home she could face more visits. She said she knew the board has no jurisdiction over it, but hoped it could work to make the neighborhood less divisive and more welcoming.

The trustee said they had no prior knowledge of what was happening and were not involved: "I'm sorry it happened to you. I think we have a better use for our police." The trustee added that things have become too divisive and hoped everyone could "pull together and work together."

In further remarks, the resident said she had since learned that both county Board of Elections commissioners had the authority to send investigators instead; that she had a productive conversation, "not a confrontation," with an official of the Westchester County Board of Elections; and that she has retained an attorney to pursue what happened to her family. She thanked the board and the other residents for their support, and acknowledged another family that had been subjected to the same thing.
